# Promise, Plan, Proof
slug: promise-plan-proof · category: sales · aliases: promise proof plan, proof promise plan

## What it is
A communication framework Hormozi uses for sales pages, content introductions, and sales calls. The promise identifies the result, the plan explains how to reach it, and the proof establishes credibility. The order can change according to which element matters most for the particular piece: a page can lead with the promise, while a content introduction can begin with proof.

## How to apply it
1. State the promise: explain the result the buyer or viewer can expect. On a sales page, put the headline promise first.
2. Explain the plan: show the method or process that leads to the result. For content, outline what the material will cover; for a pitch, explain the next steps and who does what.
3. Present proof that supports the specific promise. Match evidence to the relevant parts of the plan and the outcome being offered.
4. Demonstrate the result when possible. A product pitch can include a demonstration before moving into the plan and offer.
5. Adapt the sequence to the setting. A sales call can open with a proof, promise, plan agenda of about 15 seconds.

## When to reach for it
- A sales page needs a clear connection between its headline, method, and evidence.
- A content introduction needs to establish the benefit, credibility, and direction quickly.
- A sales call needs a brief opening agenda.
- Competitors make similar promises and you need to distinguish the offer through evidence and a believable method.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Using proof that does not demonstrate the promised result. Evidence should match the outcome, including its durability when that is part of the promise.
- Depending entirely on promises when a relevant result could be demonstrated.
- Trying to stand out only through a different promise while neglecting proof and a credible mechanism.
