# BAMFAM (Book A Meeting From A Meeting)
slug: bamfam · category: sales · aliases: book a meeting from a meeting, BAMFAM as a way of life, no-man's land

## What it is
A rule Hormozi took from his friend Sharran Srivatsaa: never end a conversation with a prospect or customer without a specific next meeting on both calendars. People are most likely to attend meetings that are actually scheduled, and a meeting is easiest to schedule while you are both already talking. Leaving it to a vague promise to circle back sends the lead into limbo, where they quietly drop you.

## How to apply it
1. Before a call ends without a close, open your calendar with them and choose a concrete date and time together.
2. Treat reluctance to commit to a next step as an objection and handle it on the spot, rather than accepting a promise to get back to you.
3. Confirm it the same way you confirm any appointment: automated plus manual reminders.
4. Make it team culture ("BAMFAM as a way of life"). No lead leaves a meeting without a next step.
5. Apply it to every stage where a second conversation is needed: proposal walk-throughs, second calls and onboarding steps.
6. **After the sale (Money Models / Retention):** every meeting is an upsell opportunity, so more booked meetings means more upsells. Make check-ins a condition of Win Your Money Back and Trial With Penalty offers (his gyms used three: nutrition orientation → supplement offer, progress check-in → membership offer, transformation review → membership or annual prepay). Check progress first, then make the offer that fits what you just heard.
7. In onboarding, set up ongoing contact so customers always know when you'll speak next. Between big touchpoints, a 1:1 reach-out every two to three weeks retains more customers.

## When to reach for it
- Multi-call sales processes where prospects vanish between the first and second call.
- Reps who end calls by promising to email some times later.
- Any handoff where a lead could fall through the cracks.
- Low upsell rates because you rarely talk to customers after the first sale.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Ending with vague promises to follow up. If you cannot book them while they are there, you almost certainly will not afterwards.
- Doing lots of unrequested work between calls instead of securing the next meeting. The insurance reps in his example built custom plans between calls and still lost prospects.

## The math
None. In his insurance-company example, this one change sharply raised second-call show rates and increased sales.
