# Affiliate Payout Tiers (pay from max allowable CAC)
slug: affiliate-payout-tiers · category: pricing · aliases: three-tier commission, maximum allowable CAC, affiliate comp plan, blended payout

## What it is
How Hormozi decides what affiliates earn. Their ongoing commitment depends on the rewards you provide, so he answers two questions: what are they paid for, and how much.

## How to apply it
1. **Decide the behaviour you want, then pay for exactly that.** Default: new customers and repeat customers. As tracking matures, you can also pay for earlier steps that reliably become sales (lead magnet downloads, booked appointments).
2. **Set your maximum allowable CAC.** From gross profit per customer, pick a target LTGP:CAC ratio; the "one part" is the most you'll pay an affiliate per customer.
3. **Tier it** instead of handing everyone the full CAC:
   - **Tier 1 (25% of max CAC):** anyone who accepts initial terms (signed up, bought product or certification).
   - **Tier 2 (50%):** once activated (finished certification, did the agreed posts/outreach, ran a launch). Doubling pay rewards activation.
   - **Tier 3 (100%):** once they sustain a performance level, e.g. keeping five customers a month on subscription.
4. **Use the leftover.** Because the blended payout sits below the max, the gap funds big contests, advertising to recruit more affiliates, and rewards for rising stars, or is simply kept.

## When to reach for it
Designing or fixing any affiliate, partner or reseller commission plan.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Giving away the whole CAC to everyone. Not all affiliates are equal; save full payouts for proven performers.
- Choosing numbers before deciding what action you are paying for.

## The math
- $200 single-use product, $40 fulfilment = $160 gross profit. Target 3:1 means $120 to the business, $40 max per customer to the affiliate.
- Tiers: $10 / $20 / $40.
- If sales come 20% from tier 1, 20% tier 2, 60% tier 3, the blended payout is $30, moving LTGP:CAC from 3:1 to 4:1. He notes a one-third cut in marketing cost often means 10–20% more net profit for the year.
