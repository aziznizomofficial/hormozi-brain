# Discount Offers (and the two-step sale)
slug: discount-offers · category: offer · aliases: discount promotions, massive discount front end, two-step sale, card on file, splinter the offer

## What it is
Discounts work like free offers: a gap between perceived value and price that drives action by itself. Hormozi dismisses small discounts (5–25% off) as margin-eaters that don't change behaviour; he means **massive** discounts of 50% or more that get people who otherwise wouldn't respond to respond. Usually you discount a piece of the offer, not the whole thing: "splinter" off a core component and sell that cheaply.

## How to apply it
**Pros:**
1. Plenty of cheap leads while staying legal where "free" claims are restricted (useful in heavily regulated industries or countries).
2. You collect some cash upfront; helpful for offsetting acquisition cost, but never your real way of funding it.
3. Prospects arrive expecting to pay, which mainly boosts your team's conviction when selling.
4. **The two-step sale (the biggest benefit).** Take the card for a cheap first step; paid appointments get far fewer no-shows (he puts show rates at 85–90%+), and you can charge a fee for missing a follow-up.
5. **Smooth upsells:** with the card on file, the next purchase is just "use the card we have?" Removing the card-out moment lifts sales (think one-click buying).
Example flow: ad, opt-in, call to sell a $19 assessment and book it, show up and get value, book a follow-up, sell a $2,100 ten-week program there.

Discounts shown in four different ways can get different responses; test the display.

## When to reach for it
Well-understood services with known price ranges (dentists, chiropractors, gyms, haircuts); time-costly services where no-shows hurt; regulated markets. Discounts can boost take rates on back-end offers too.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Discounting your core offer regularly, which trains buyers to wait for sales (the retailer model is a double-edged sword).
- Treating discount buyers as customers. They're qualified leads to upsell; Groupon complaints came from businesses without structure to move buyers into the core service.
- Discounting something people don't understand; with no reference price, a discount means nothing.

## The math
$19 entry offer leading to a $2,100 program; 85–90%+ show rate on paid appointments.
