# Pay Less Now or Pay More Later
slug: pay-less-now-or-pay-more-later · category: money-model · aliases: pay now vs pay later, bill-me-later trial, speed bonus, early-bird offer

## What it is
An attraction offer that gives two ways to buy. **Pay later** means the customer puts a card down for $0 and is billed full price at a set date unless they cancel after meeting the conditions of a satisfaction guarantee. **Pay now** gets a 20–50% discount plus bonuses, but no guarantee. The pay-later option removes the buyer's risk, which lets you advertise "free." Once someone accepts it, you offer the pay-now deal, and their card is already on file.

## How to apply it
1. Promise one clear yes/no result you can deliver within the window, and make it measurable (e.g., rate the pain from 1 to 10 before and after).
2. Make the guarantee conditional. Cancelling is allowed only if they did their part (attended, showed up, submitted data), and those conditions should be what gets them the most value.
3. After they accept pay-later, offer pay-now with the discount and bonuses.
4. Have a larger follow-up offer ready (an intensive, a monthly service, a subscription). At events, mention it early so demand builds.
5. For recurring businesses: pay a higher rate starting in 30 days, or lock in a lower rate for good by paying today.

## When to reach for it
Digital products, workshops, short services and physical-product trials, anywhere a quick, visible result can be shown. It is easy to train salespeople to sell.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Promising more than you can deliver in the window, which leads to cancellations.
- If more than about 10% of pay-later customers cancel, you over-promised, the guarantee conditions are too loose, or the price is too high.
- Keep the two options balanced. If most pick pay-later, make pay-now sweeter; if most pick pay-now, pull back.

## The math
The reading course that taught him this: $0 today and $297 billed tomorrow unless reading speed doubled, versus $97 paid now with recordings included (p. 66). Other examples: a workshop at $0→$500 later or $299 now (p. 67), and hedges at $0→$599 later or $369 now (p. 67).
