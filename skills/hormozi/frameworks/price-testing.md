# Price Testing (Raise Prices)
slug: price-testing · category: pricing · aliases: raise prices, increase price, price-to-conversion test, start low then go up, raise price in stages

## What it is
Price is the strongest lever on gross profit, because every extra dollar goes straight to the bottom line. Hormozi tests pricing more than anything else and finds the right price is usually higher than owners expect. The goal is not the most sales but the most money: the price where conversion rate × lifetime gross profit peaks. He once bought a company, doubled the price and changed nothing else, and the business tripled.

## How to apply it
1. Start new offers cheap enough to get many yeses, so you learn, fix delivery and prove demand.
2. Once the offer is reliable, raise the price about 20% every 10 sales or so until sales drop sharply, then go back to the best-performing point.
3. For each new price, calculate the break-even conversion rate against the old price and track real conversion. If you beat break-even, keep the new price and test again.
4. Test every quarter. He cites ProfitWell research linking profitability to how often companies test price.
5. Watch churn as well as conversion. Higher prices can raise churn (seen across Skool), so judge price by total return.
6. Match the pricing model to the value. Don't force recurring billing onto something with one-time value.
7. Plan tests in advance (this price, for this many people). Never cut price on the spot out of fear of losing a sale.

## When to reach for it
Thin margins, a sold-out or capacity-limited business, prices unchanged for years, or you've never seen a customer flinch at your price.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Never testing, which leaves the business under-monetized for good. That costs more than a few months of awkward tests.
- Treating ad-hoc discounting as testing. Customers talk, and inconsistent prices damage trust.
- If early buyers paid a price that turns out to be too high, add bonuses or refund the difference.

## The math
- A 10%-margin business that raises price 20% with unchanged sales roughly triples profit (LTV Playbook p. 11).
- Retention table (100 clicks): $10 → 5 sales, 10% churn, $500 total. $20 → 4 sales, 10% churn, $800 total. $100 → 2 sales, 33% churn, $600 total. $20 wins, so test between $39 and $59 next (Retention pp. 11–12).
- If doubling the price loses only 20% of deals, do it (Retention p. 12).
- Keep raising until the extra money from yeses no longer covers the lost nos (Money Models p. 172).
