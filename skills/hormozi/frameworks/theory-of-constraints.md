# Theory of Constraints
slug: theory-of-constraints · category: scaling · aliases: theory of constraint

## What it is
A growth principle Hormozi uses to decide where effort belongs. A business expands until one bottleneck limits its output. Removing that bottleneck permits further growth until another constraint takes over. Improving other parts of the business does not increase total output while the limiting stage remains unchanged.

He defines the constraint as the point where improvement produces the greatest return on effort. The practical task is to identify which problem matters most now, rather than treat every problem as equally urgent.

## How to apply it
1. Start by determining whether demand or the capacity to fulfill it limits the business.
2. Ask what prevents the business from growing 10x. Use the answer to identify the bottleneck to address.
3. Concentrate attention and resources on the limiting stage. Let areas that do not constrain output remain good enough.
4. Check whether resolving the suspected constraint produces growth. If it does not, revisit the diagnosis; Hormozi treats that as evidence that the wrong constraint was selected.
5. Identify the next bottleneck and redirect effort. More demand can expose insufficient capacity, while more capacity can expose insufficient demand.

## When to reach for it
- Growth has stalled despite continued activity.
- There are many problems and no clear priority.
- Improvements in individual departments fail to increase overall output.
- A previous growth fix has created a different limitation.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Increasing capacity in a stage that does not limit the business.
- Perfecting areas that are already good enough while the bottleneck persists.
- Staying focused on the previous constraint after the limiting factor changes.
- Spending attention on enjoyable work instead of the area where effort would return the most.
