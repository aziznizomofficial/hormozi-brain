# What-Who-When Framework
slug: what-who-when-ad-framework · category: marketing · aliases: ad angles, value equation in copy, carrots and sticks, get them interested

## What it is
Hormozi's method for writing an ad's value section: the middle that explains why the viewer should care. Examining the value equation from three perspectives expands the range of angles available.

## How to apply it
**What (eight elements).** Know how your thing delivers each of the four value drivers and helps them escape each opposite:
- Dream outcome vs. the nightmare of going without.
- Perceived likelihood of success (explain away past failures, proof from people like them, authority, guarantees) vs. the risk of carrying on as they are.
- Speed vs. how slow their current path is.
- Ease vs. the effort, skill and sacrifice they would need without you (or have already wasted).

**Who.** People are status driven, and status is granted by others. Describe the benefits from the eyes of spouse, kids, parents, colleagues, bosses, friends, rivals and competitors. Each viewpoint applied to each value driver is a new story.

**When.** Walk the prospect through their own timeline: what their choices caused in the past, what they cost now, where they lead. Show the bad future first, then contrast it with the good present and future if they buy.

Combine them: a specific person (who) noticing a specific benefit (what) at a specific time (when).

## When to reach for it
Writing ad copy, sales pages, VSLs, or content angles; any time you run dry on "reasons to care."

## Mistakes he warns about
- Only writing from the buyer's own perspective, missing the status angles.
- Only talking about the present moment.
- Thinking long and short ads are different species: the only difference is how many angles you have room for. Keep the callout and CTA the same.

## The math
4 drivers x 2 directions = 8 "whats"; multiply by every "who" and three "whens" for a large angle bank.
