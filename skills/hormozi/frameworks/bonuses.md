# Bonuses (Stacking Value Instead of Discounting)
slug: bonuses · category: offer · aliases: value stack, but wait there's more, bonus stacking, other people's products as bonuses

## What it is
The same offer is worth more when broken into its components and presented one piece at a time than as a single lump. You anchor the price to the core offer, then keep adding named pieces until the value exceeds the price enough that declining feels unreasonable. Adding value closes deals from a position of strength; cutting price teaches buyers your prices are negotiable.

## How to apply it
Selling one-on-one: ask for the sale first. If they say yes, reveal the bonuses afterwards as a pleasant surprise. If they say no, add a bonus aimed at their stated obstacle and ask again, repeating as needed.

For each bonus:
1. Give it a name that carries a benefit.
2. Explain how it relates to their problem, what it is, how you created it, and how it makes things faster or easier.
3. Show proof it works and paint the picture of life after using it.
4. Put a price tag on it and justify it.
5. Prefer tools, templates and checklists over more training (less effort, more value).
6. Aim each bonus at a specific doubt or at the next problem they will hit.
7. Make the bonuses' total value exceed the core offer's.
8. Add scarcity or urgency to the bonuses themselves.

Other people's products: ask non-competing businesses to donate services in exchange for exposure to your customers, and negotiate a group discount plus a referral commission.

## When to reach for it
- You are tempted to discount to close.
- Your deliverables are valuable but invisible to the buyer.
- Prospects voice specific "but what about..." objections.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Discounting the core offer.
- Burying standout items in the main package; pull out the distinct, high-wow pieces (short checklists, maps) and present them as bonuses.
- Letting recordings and assets go to waste; record every workshop and keep a vault of bonus material.

## The math
His pain-clinic illustration: partner freebies (adjustments, orthotics discounts, gym passes and so on) can be worth more than the $400 offer itself, and negotiated referral fees could add roughly $350 of profit per sale. His supplement company gave clients' customers 30% off and paid the referring athlete 40% of net sales.
