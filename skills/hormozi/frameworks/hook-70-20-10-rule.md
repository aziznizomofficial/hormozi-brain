# 70-20-10 Hook Rule
slug: hook-70-20-10-rule · category: marketing · aliases: 70/20/10 innovation rule, hook bank, winner adjacent, let the data do the teaching

## What it is
Hormozi's system for finding and producing hooks, borrowed from Google's 70-20-10 split of innovation effort. Most of each batch reuses proven hooks, a smaller share adapts ideas that worked somewhere else, and a small slice goes to wild experiments.

## How to apply it
1. **Build a hook bank.** A spreadsheet with a tab per platform and columns for name, hook, views and link. Review your all-time best before every content or ad session.
2. **70% core:** reuse hooks that already won, yours or other people's. His best tweets become the openers of short videos and captions.
3. **20% "winner adjacent":** take outliers from other niches or platforms (via outlier-finding tools, or by searching your topics and sorting by performance), screenshot them into a deck, and note how you could repackage each formula.
4. **10% big new ideas:** new visual formats and odd concepts. Log the losers so you do not repeat them. Winners move into the 70% bucket.
5. **Record many hooks per piece:** about 10 hooks for each ad body, 3–4 alternate openings for long videos (the team picks one in editing), a split test on every email campaign, and short videos that open with a winning tweet and then expand on it.
6. **If you have no history,** make a lot of content, then study your top 10% and bottom 10%. Do more of the first and less of the second, and keep repeating.

## When to reach for it
- You do not know what content to make next. In his view the hook decides the content, not the other way round.
- Ad or content results swing wildly from week to week.
- A team needs a repeatable creative process instead of waiting for inspiration.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Waiting for a teacher. Your own data is the teacher.
- Spending most effort on novelty. Proven hooks keep revenue stable enough to make payroll every month.

## The math
Batch allocation: 70% proven hooks / 20% variations of those hooks / 10% completely new. Checklist version: look at your top 5 performers, build 70% of the next batch on them, 20% on permutations (words and visuals), 10% on new hooks.
