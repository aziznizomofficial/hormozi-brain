# Anchor Upsell
slug: anchor-upsell · category: money-model · aliases: price anchor, sticker-shock upsell, The Gasp, suit-shop play

## What it is
Show the premium option first, at roughly 5–10x the price of your main offer. When the customer balks, offer the main offer as a cheaper but perfectly good alternative. After the anchor, the main offer looks like a bargain, so more people buy it and many spend more than they planned. A few will buy the premium outright, and those sales bring outsized profit. Hormozi walked into a suit shop with a $500 budget, saw a $16,000 suit, and left happy having spent about $2,500.

## How to apply it
1. **Present the anchor** and actually sell it, as though you want them to buy it.
2. **Get the gasp.** A visible shock means the anchor is working. A bigger gasp usually means a bigger purchase.
3. **Come to the rescue.** Ask whether they care about what makes it premium (the designer, the extras).
4. **Present the main offer**, already pulled and ready. Keep the **primary** features the same and change only the **secondary** or premium ones.
5. **Ask how they want to pay** (which card). If they don't gasp, they may accept the premium price, so ask for payment on it.

## When to reach for it
Most buyers take your core offer and nobody takes the premium tier. You suspect some customers would pay far more. You're losing big spenders because you have nothing bigger to sell them.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Treating the anchor as fake. If you rush past it, the customer never considers it, and you lose trust.
- Anchoring with something you'd hate to deliver. Build a premium offer you'd happily fulfill. His friend tripled profit after making the anchor real.
- Not having a premium option at all. Offering $100 to someone with a $1,000 budget costs you far more than the reverse ($900 lost versus $100).

## The math
- Anchor at 5–10x the main offer. The main offer then delivers the primary features at about a fifth of the price (p. 99).
- Examples: lawn care at $1,000/week premium vs $200/week main; a newsletter at $199/month vs $19/month (pp. 97–98).
