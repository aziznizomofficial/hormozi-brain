# The Many-Sided Die
slug: many-sided-die · category: mindset · aliases: keep rolling, you cannot lose if you do not quit, win or learn

## What it is
The closing parable of $100M Leads, which Hormozi says carried him through his hardest stretches. Two players each get a die whose side count they can't see (one has 20 sides, the other 200); each die has a single green face. Landing green turns another red face green and you roll again; landing red changes nothing and you roll again. The game ends only when you stop, and stopping is losing.

## How to apply it
- Treat every advertising attempt (an ad, a campaign, an outreach batch, a new platform) as a roll. Early rolls are mostly red; that's expected, not a verdict.
- Each green makes the next green more likely: skill and proof accumulate, so success becomes the rule rather than the exception.
- Ignore other players' dice. You can't see whether someone is on roll 100 or roll 100,000, or how many sides they started with; you only see today's results.
- Spend your time rolling, not watching and complaining. The "lucky" ones are lucky largely because they keep playing.

## When to reach for it
After a string of failed tests; when comparing yourself to competitors who seem to win easily; when tempted to switch strategies after a few losses. Pairs with his advice to pick one method and stick with it (see [[lead-getters]]) and his "win or learn" framing of test budgets (see [[more-better-new]]).

## Mistakes he warns about
- The friend's pattern: roll a few times, blame the die (the game is rigged, they got an easier one), complain about how long it took, and quit just before a streak.
- Assuming a bad start means a worse die. With enough rolls, which die you got stops mattering.

## The math
Only two outcomes are guaranteed: additional rolls build skill, and quitting brings defeat. His point is that continuing to play keeps defeat from becoming final. (p. 381)
