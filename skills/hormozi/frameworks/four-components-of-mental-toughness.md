# Four Components of Mental Toughness
slug: four-components-of-mental-toughness · category: mindset · aliases: mental toughness, mental toughness model

## What it is
His behavioral model for understanding how adversity affects actions that serve long-term goals. Greater toughness means adverse events are less likely to push behavior away from those goals. He separates it into tolerance, fortitude, resilience, and adaptability. The components must be considered together: someone can react strongly to a setback yet recover quickly. He emphasizes the definitions more than the labels.

## How to apply it
1. Assess tolerance: how much adversity occurs before behavior changes?
2. Assess fortitude: once behavior changes, how large is the departure from the usual baseline?
3. Assess resilience: how quickly does behavior return to baseline?
4. Assess adaptability: does behavior rebound above its previous baseline?
5. Use these distinctions to identify the response that needs development. Treat toughness as a set of trainable behaviors rather than a fixed identity.
6. Attend to sleep and food. He notes that being underslept or unfed reduces tolerance and that better physical conditions support the components of toughness.

## When to reach for it
- Repeated setbacks disrupt actions that support a long-term goal.
- A general instruction to become tougher gives no useful direction.
- Someone reacts strongly but recovers quickly, making a single toughness label misleading.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Treating toughness as something a person either has or lacks. His model places it on a continuum.
- Judging the whole model from one component. A large behavioral swing can coexist with fast recovery.
- Giving vague instructions to toughen up instead of identifying specific behaviors to develop.
- Ignoring the effects of sleep and food on the ability to respond to adversity.
