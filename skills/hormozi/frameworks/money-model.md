# Money Model
slug: money-model · category: money-model · aliases: $100M Money Model, sequence of offers, four offer types, Get Cash / Get More Cash / Get The Most Cash

## What it is
A Money Model is the deliberate order in which a business makes offers: what it offers, when, and how, so each customer pays back their own acquisition and delivery cost fast. Hormozi's bar is covering the cost to get and serve a customer within 30 days; the $100M version earns enough in those 30 days to fund two or more additional customers, which removes cash as the limit on growth. Every model combines four categories of offers: attraction offers, upsells, downsells, and continuity offers.

## How to apply it
1. **Stage I, Get Cash:** install one attraction offer (free or discounted entry) and advertise it until strangers reliably become customers.
2. **Stage II, Get More Cash:** add an upsell for the problem the first purchase exposes, then a downsell for the people who say no (adjust the payment arrangement or the deliverables).
3. **Stage III, Get The Most Cash:** end with a continuity offer, ideally with a prepaid block of time that auto-renews.
4. Build in order: first get customers reliably, then make them self-funding, then make them fund others, then raise long-term value, then pour money into ads.
5. Fill gaps with affiliate products you don't deliver yourself, and create many ways to offer the same product rather than many products.

## When to reach for it
Ads "don't work" because payback takes months; one-product "front end only" businesses; owners floating the company on credit; any plan to scale paid acquisition.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Installing the whole model at once. Perfect one offer until it is boring, then add the next; measure in quarters, not weeks.
- Starting more businesses to have more things to sell.
- Hard selling. Make offers when the customer feels the problem, and accept a no.
- Lying or skipping legal review, especially around the word "free."
- Keeping money from someone who asks for a refund. Give it back.

## The math
- Credit cards give roughly 30 days of interest-free float, so recovering CAC inside 30 days lets you recycle the same money into the next customer (p. 24).
- If customer value, customer count and speed of payment each double, growth compounds to 8x; tripling each gives 27x (p. 24).
- Rental car example: a $19/day booking became about $100/day through five sequential offers (pp. 21–23).
