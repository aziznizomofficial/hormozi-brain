# Follow-Up Cadence (Volume Pillar)
slug: follow-up-cadence · category: leads · aliases: volume pillar, volume negates luck, double dial, reminder sequence, 7+ touches

## What it is
The fourth pillar of lead nurture: reach out more times before giving up. Most attempts fail for ordinary reasons. The timing was bad, the lead meant to answer and forgot, the message got buried, or they were not ready then but are now. A lead who opted in asked to be contacted, so persistence is ethical as well as profitable. Hormozi splits volume into three parts: getting the appointment scheduled, reminding people to show, and booking the next appointment while they are in the current one (see BAMFAM).

## How to apply it
**Scheduling cadence (front-loaded, since each passing day lowers the odds):**
1. Call within 5 minutes of opt-in.
2. No answer: dial again straight away. Many phones let a second call from an unknown number ring through.
3. Leave a voicemail, then text right after.
4. Repeat the double-dial plus text twice more that same day, spacing the attempts a few hours apart.
5. Days 2–3: call twice (early and late) and text after the second call.
6. The next four days: call and text once a day. This also catches people who answer on weekends.
7. After a week, move them to long-term nurture (free value with soft asks). If they re-engage, start again from the top.

**Reminders:**
- Automated, and openly labelled as automated: an instant confirmation (time, date, who they will meet, and the number or area code you will call from, in their time zone), then 24h, 12h and 3h before.
- Send manually using a real phone: once the previous night, again that morning, and once more roughly an hour beforehand.
- Send several short messages rather than one long block. Keep the conversation going by acknowledging what they said, complimenting them, then asking the next question, and use that to qualify or pull the appointment earlier.

## When to reach for it
- Reps give up after one or two attempts.
- Many leads are never reached at all.
- No-shows from people who simply forgot.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Stopping after the first attempt, which he says nearly half of salespeople do.
- Pretending automated reminders come from a person. People accept reminders but resent being deceived.
- Ignoring local laws on calling and texting.

## The math
He cites an average of 1.3 attempts before salespeople give up, with 44% stopping after one (InsideSales.com, RAIN Group). His target is 5–7+ touches across channels. He says an auto-dialer makes a team 2–3x more efficient, and that showing the area code you will call from can double pick-ups.
