# Waived Fee Offer
slug: waived-fee-offer · category: money-model · aliases: waived fee continuity, setup fee waived with commitment, penalty-avoidance continuity

## What it is
Offer two options: month-to-month with a large startup fee (typically 3–5x the monthly rate), or a 12-month commitment with the fee waived. Anyone who commits and cancels early pays the waived fee, which puts them in the same position as if they'd chosen month-to-month. Flexibility becomes the expensive choice, so most people commit. After the early months, leaving costs about as much as staying, so they stay. Hormozi learned it from a high-ticket seller who ran a low-effort, high-cash business on this alone.

## How to apply it
1. Set the commitment length. A year or longer works best, especially for results that take time (SEO, investing, weight loss).
2. Set the fee at 3–5x the monthly price. For more cash up front, use 1.5–3x so more people choose month-to-month and pay it.
3. Justify the fee honestly: starting a client costs money. If they only want to test, they cover that cost. If they commit, you cover it.
4. Have them initial that they understand the fee applies if they leave early.
5. Once they complete the term, drop the fee. They've earned a free exit.
6. Optional twist for motivation: if they quit early, the fee is donated to a cause they dislike.

## When to reach for it
Businesses with real onboarding costs, slow-to-show results, or a need for predictable recurring revenue. It fits where a traditional contract makes more sense than a reward-based offer.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Using the fee to trap people in a bad product. If more than about 5% want out early, fix the product. The aim is a nudge, not handcuffs.
- Commitments shorter than a year weaken the effect.

## The math
At $1,000/month with a $5,000 fee: Option A is $5,000 plus $1,000 for month one, then $1,000/month, cancel anytime. Option B is $1,000/month for 12 months with the fee waived, owed only if they quit early (pp. 162–163). A bigger fee means more commitments. A smaller fee means more up-front cash.
