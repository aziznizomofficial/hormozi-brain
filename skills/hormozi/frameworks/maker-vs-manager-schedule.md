# Maker vs Manager Schedule
slug: maker-vs-manager-schedule · category: operations · aliases: maker versus manager

## What it is
A way to organize time around two different kinds of work. Maker work needs long, uninterrupted blocks for important tasks with little immediate urgency. Manager work fits into short conversations and decisions across the business. Hormozi treats these as a continuum: a person's role can require both, but mixing them throughout the day disrupts focused work.

Maker blocks last four to six hours, about 14 per week; manager schedules contain 5-15 minute slots, 100+ per week. A short meeting can consume much more than its scheduled duration because anticipation and interruptions break concentration.

## How to apply it
1. Identify how much of the role requires making versus managing.
2. Ask makers to describe their ideal working schedule, then protect the uninterrupted blocks they need.
3. Batch necessary coordination into minimal recurring meeting windows.
4. Separate maker and manager work across days or substantial blocks. Hormozi describes using four maker days and one manager day on Mondays, while his wife uses the reverse to run daily operations.
5. Account for the interruption caused by arranging a meeting as well as attending it.

## When to reach for it
- Meetings fragment the time needed to produce substantial work.
- Managers need alignment, but their requests repeatedly interrupt makers.
- A role combines focused production with responsibility for other people.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Treating a maker's empty calendar as spare availability.
- Measuring a meeting's cost only by its duration. Reaching flow can take about 30 to 45 minutes, and anticipation can pull attention away before the meeting begins.
- Ignoring the double disruption of the coordination exchange and the scheduled session.
