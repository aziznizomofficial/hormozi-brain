# Affiliate Integration (launch, then integrate)
slug: affiliate-integration · category: leads · aliases: keep them advertising, lead magnet bundling, affiliate LTGP:CAC, launch then integrate

## What it is
Step 6 of building an affiliate army: turning a one-time launch into leads that flow for years. The ideal is selling an affiliate once and receiving engaged leads for life. You do that by weaving your product into their offer. Hormozi's three options, easiest to hardest:

## How to apply it
1. **They give your lead magnet away with their purchase.** It raises the value of their offer (so they can charge more and win more leads) and you upsell. Use a sample/trial, a problem-revealing assessment, or step one of a multi-step solution. His version: gyms gave every new member a free nutrition consult, where supplements were sold.
2. **They sell your lead magnet** (a discounted service, a workshop, a book, step one) and keep the cash. You profit by selling your core offer afterwards and don't have to split it. Gyms charged for an hour-long nutrition consult and kept the money.
3. **They sell your core offer directly.** They earn a bigger share of lifetime gross profit; you just deliver. Payment options: split upfront cash, cash for a period, or forever. He prefers paying forever with no cap, so affiliates keep caring about retention.
He runs option 1 as a twice-yearly event and option 3 continuously.

**Case studies:** a $50M business-formation firm offers free LLC setup to customers of people who train new entrepreneurs, then sells bookkeeping and tax services, with no paid ads. Prestige Labs gets gym owners to run a free 28-day challenge, then sell supplements at a nutrition orientation for each new member. Chiropractors run posture workshops at gyms (gym keeps the ticket money), then get adjustments bundled into new memberships.

## When to reach for it
After a successful launch, when affiliate sales fade; designing a partner program for durability.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Stopping at the launch.
- Offers that don't fit the partner's business. Make it so good they'd feel silly declining.
- Declaring affiliates "can't work" for you instead of making them work.

## The math
Returns compare cost to recruit an affiliate against gross profit from all the customers they send. Example: $4,000 to acquire an affiliate; $10k/month sales for 12 months = $120k; 25% COGS = $30k; 40% of $90k gross profit paid out = $36k; $54k left = 12.5:1. Below 3:1, recruit cheaper (ads, offer, sales), activate more (launch), or make each worth more (integration). Plan: get 10–20 affiliates, fix the kinks, turn their results into lead magnets for recruiting more.
