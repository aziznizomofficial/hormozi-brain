# Marketing Machine (Customer-Generated Ads)
slug: marketing-machine · category: marketing · aliases: GL marketing machine, hit-by-a-bus question, customer-generated ads, proof capture system, remove the founder from the ads

## What it is
A checklist of recurring processes that turn what a business already does (calls, events, communities, support chats, reviews, awards) into a steady weekly supply of ads, without the founder on camera. Hormozi built it after bankers called Gym Launch unsellable because revenue depended on his face. Within a year the top ads were customer-made, and the company later sold for $46.2M. His reasoning: a business that relies on its founder is worth less and is riskier to buy, customers are the real heroes of the story, and prospects want to see results from real people anyway.

## How to apply it
1. **Ask the key question:** if a bus struck you today, how would the company continue growing the next day? List everything you personally do, then build systems for it.
2. **Switch on the nodes that fit your business:** lifecycle ads, social media scrape, events, communication/community/review scrapes, bonus and award unlocks, competitions, and delivery "reveal" moments.
3. **Start with one.** His suggested order for beginners: competition → chat scrape → community scrape → review scrape → social scrape → bonus unlocks → award unlocks → events → lifecycle. It runs from the biggest quick influx and the least editing to the most production work.
4. **Put it on the marketing team's start-of-week checklist.** Each week collect: names of customers with great results, scraped social media, chat screenshots, community wins, new 5-star reviews, bonus-unlock and award submissions, and customer reaction videos.
5. **Schedule the big bursts:** two events and one competition a year, spaced out, bring a large influx roughly every twelve weeks.
6. **Turn every asset into ads** and use it in sales pitches, webinars, VSLs and outreach. A folder of assets gets you nothing until it is running.

## When to reach for it
- The founder is the face of every ad and the business will not sell or scale without them.
- Creative has dried up, or ad performance depends on one person's energy.
- You have happy customers but little captured proof.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Spending years "dancing on camera," which makes the business less valuable and usually works worse than customer proof.
- Feeling overwhelmed by the full list. He says one is better than none.
- Having too few customers to start. First work for free (warm outreach) to get results worth capturing.

## The math
When he later reviewed Gym Launch's top 50 ads, 40 (80%) did not feature him.
