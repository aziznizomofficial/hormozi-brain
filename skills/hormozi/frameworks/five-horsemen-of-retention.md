# The 5 Horsemen of Retention
slug: five-horsemen-of-retention · category: retention · aliases: five horsemen, gym retention framework, common factors analysis

## What it is
Five retention habits Hormozi found in 2019 when Gym Launch clients were losing members as fast as they signed them up. He borrowed a Department of Defense consulting method: ask many experts, collect all their answers, and keep only what they share. His "experts" were the gym owners with under 3% monthly churn for six months. Five factors were common to all of them: track attendance, reach out twice a week, send handwritten cards, hold member events, and require exit interviews.

## How to apply it
1. **Track attendance.** Members who came three or more times a week stayed. When attendance slipped to two, a quick contact usually brought them back before they cancelled.
2. **Reach out twice a week.** Praise participation and progress, and fix small problems.
3. **Handwritten cards** at sign-up, at the 3-, 6- and 12-month milestones (with a referral ask), and on holidays and birthdays. Write them yourself.
4. **Member events** on an internal calendar every 21, 42 or 63 days: regular for you, unpredictable for them. Send handwritten invitations. Events cut churn and bring referrals.
5. **Exit interviews.** Tell members at onboarding that cancelling requires a conversation, then treat every cancellation notice as the trigger to book one.
6. For any new problem, reuse the method: interview the top performers and adopt what they have in common.

## When to reach for it
Local membership businesses (gyms, studios, clubs) with high churn, or any business where attendance or usage shows whether someone is still engaged.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Panicking in month one. Churn usually **rises** at first as people who were already leaving, or still being billed without using the service, cancel once reminded. He calls this shaking the tree. It then falls.
- Doing only some of the five. The results came from doing all of them.

## The math
- At 15% monthly churn a gym loses about 83% of members in a year, so a 250-member gym needs 208 new sign-ups a year just to stay flat (p. 4).
- Typical rollout: month 1 went from 10% to 15%, month 2 to 7%, month 3 to 3% (p. 8).
- Exit interviews save about half of those who attend. If half attend, churn falls about 25%, roughly a 33% LTV increase (p. 7).
- Going from 9% to 3% churn is a 3.3x LTV increase (p. 8).
