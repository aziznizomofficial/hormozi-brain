# LTGP to CAC Ratio
slug: ltgp-to-cac · category: money-model · aliases: LTGP:CAC, lifetime gross profit, 3 to 1, ads problem vs business model problem

## What it is
Hormozi's main efficiency gauge for advertising: lifetime gross profit per customer divided by cost to acquire one. He deliberately uses gross profit, not "lifetime value" revenue, because gross profit is the actual money that funds acquisition, rent and payroll. When he says he is "getting 3 to 1," this is the ratio he means.

## How to apply it
1. **LTGP** = everything a customer spends over their lifetime minus the cost of delivering it.
2. **CAC** = all you spend to win one customer.
3. Above 1:1 your acquisition is profitable; below it you lose money.
4. Two levers: lower CAC (better ads, targeting, sales) or raise LTGP (better business model: price, upsells, retention). He prefers pulling both.
5. **Diagnose which lever to pull** using your industry's average CAC. If your CAC is within 3x of the average, the ads are fine; work on the business model. If it is more than 3x the average, work on the advertising.

## When to reach for it
Any time someone says "ads don't work," or a business stalls while trying to scale acquisition.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Thinking you have bad ads when you actually have a weak business model. Competitors in one industry usually pay surprisingly similar amounts per customer; the winners simply make more per customer.
- Pursuing endless cost reductions. Zero is the lowest costs can go, but earnings per customer have no ceiling; beyond a point, reducing CAC resembles expecting savings alone to produce a billion.
- Confusing a sales problem with an ad problem: a portfolio company spent $150k over twelve weeks, had the right, qualified leads on the phone, blamed the ads and quit. The real issue was sales; he estimates it cost them about $30M in enterprise value.

## The math
- $15 item, $5 to deliver = $10 gross profit; ten purchases = $100 LTGP.
- His observed pattern (explicitly not a rule): every portfolio company struggling to scale was under 3:1, and they took off once pushed above it.
- A billion-dollar LTGP survives terrible ads; a one-cent LTGP fails with perfect ones.
