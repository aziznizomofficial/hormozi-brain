# 10x the 10%
slug: ten-x-the-ten-percent · category: pricing · aliases: 10x the 10% rule, ultra-premium offer, premium tier, always have something bigger to sell

## What it is
If 10% of your customers pay 10x your normal price, revenue doubles. You've already paid to acquire those customers, so nearly all of the extra money (minus delivery) is profit. Hormozi's argument is that a single fixed-price offer puts a ceiling on what any customer can spend, while wealthy buyers are more common than they look (he cites roughly 1 in 9 Americans being a millionaire) and will pay far more if the value backs it up.

## How to apply it
1. Build an ultra-premium version from the things that don't scale: personal access to you or senior people, a team serving one client, customization, speed, priority, longer duration, status perks, white-glove experiences, in-person access, networking with top customers, insider resources.
2. Price it at 10x to 50x your normal transaction, far enough above your other products that they aren't compared.
3. Limit the number of buyers. Scarcity is part of what attracts high spenders, and it protects delivery.
4. Offer it to existing customers first, through a [[fast-cash-play]] campaign or by presenting it first on sales calls as an anchor.
5. Actually deliver. You'll work harder for these customers, so price high enough that you're glad to.

## When to reach for it
You have one price point, buyers never flinch at your price, you lose big spenders because you have nothing bigger, or you need profit without more lead flow.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Timidity. The worst case is selling only a few, which is still found money.
- A fake premium offer. If it isn't worth the price, you lose trust. Its value should be just as startling to buyers as its price.
- Having no premium option at all. Offering $100 to someone with a $1,000 budget costs you more than offering $1,000 to someone with a $100 budget, and he calls it one of his most expensive lessons.

## The math
- 10% of customers × 10x price = +100% revenue, and depending on the mix it can be 2–4x (Fast Cash p. 8).
- Overlooking greater spending capacity: a $100 offer leaves $900 uncollected from someone prepared to spend $1,000; a $1,000 offer to someone with only $100 forfeits that $100 (Money Models p. 97).
- Example ultra-premium offers: a $10,000 one-year 1:1 fitness transformation, a $50,000/year B2B "get famous" content package, and a recurring offer at monthly × 12 with three bonuses (Fast Cash pp. 15–16).
