# More, Better, Cheaper Leads
slug: more-better-cheaper-leads · category: leads · aliases: two ways to grow, the lead goal, getting more customers

## What it is
Hormozi's framing for why leads matter. A business can only grow in two ways: acquire more customers, or make each customer worth more. $100M Leads deals only with the first lever. Getting more customers comes down to four goals for your lead flow: more of them, better quality, lower cost, and reliability (meaning they come from several sources, not one fragile channel).

## How to apply it
- Decide which of the two growth levers you are pulling. If the problem is "not enough customers", work on leads; if customers are cheap to get but worth little, work on offer/money model instead.
- Audit your current lead flow against the four goals: volume, quality, cost, and number of independent sources.
- Treat advertising (making your offer known) as the tool that forces strangers to find you instead of waiting for them.

## When to reach for it
- The owner says "business is slow" or "we need growth" without diagnosing which lever is broken.
- Revenue depends on a single referral partner or one ad account.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Trying to skip to "making money" without having all three pieces: something to sell (offer), people to sell it to (leads), and a way to get them to buy (sales).
- Under-advertising: his diagnosis of most lead problems is simply that not enough people know you exist.

## The math
All else equal, doubling leads doubles the business. He points to advertising's forgiveness: enough lead flow lets you survive a weak product or weak sales while you fix them. His own claimed lifetime return on ad spend is 36:1 (p. 11).
