# Decoy Offer
slug: decoy-offer · category: money-model · aliases: Decoys, free vs premium, "free stuff or lasting results?" (p. 56), 5-Day $5 VIP pass

## What it is
An attraction offer that advertises a free or cheap, stripped-down version of what you sell. When a lead responds, you show that version next to a much richer premium version, and the contrast makes the premium one the obvious choice. Anyone who takes the decoy is still a customer you can upgrade later, so almost everyone closes on one option or the other. It depends on knowing better than the customer what actually gets results.

## How to apply it
1. Build the decoy from your premium offer with fewer components, older versions, less personalization and **no guarantee**. It only has to get people to engage.
2. Advertise the outcome (e.g., "21-Day Transformation"), not features. Details belong in the sales conversation.
3. Present the premium offer first if you can. If the lead asks about the decoy, ask whether they came for free stuff or for lasting results. On "results," go straight to premium. Otherwise, show both and ask which one gets them to their goal faster.
4. Sell the premium with genuine enthusiasm and an assumptive, matter-of-fact close.
5. Optional: give decoy takers one or two low-cost premium extras as a surprise, to build goodwill before later upsells.

## When to reach for it
Lead costs have risen and your existing offer has lost its appeal. You want inexpensive customer acquisition and have a clearly superior paid tier. Salespeople can learn it easily.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Too little contrast. If money isn't coming in fast, widen the gap between the two options.
- Brushing past the decoy. People who choose it are still leads to upsell.
- Leading ads with features instead of the dream outcome.

## The math
- The same discount can be framed four ways (percent off, dollar amount off, free months, package price). $900 for a $100/month year can be pitched as 25% off, $300 off, 3 months free, or "a year for $900." Test which framing converts (p. 55).
- Free offers bring more leads. Discounted ones bring fewer but show up more often, so use a discount when no-shows are expensive (p. 55).
- His gyms' version: about 8 in 10 people chose the $399 premium over the free option (p. 52).
