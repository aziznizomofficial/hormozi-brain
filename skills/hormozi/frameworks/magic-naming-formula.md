# MAGIC Naming Formula (and Offer Fatigue)
slug: magic-naming-formula · category: marketing · aliases: M-A-G-I-C, offer wrapper, naming your offer, offer fatigue, change the wrapper not the offer

## What it is
The name is the wrapping paper on your offer. A better name gets more people to notice and respond to the exact same deliverables, and renaming lets you refresh a tired offer without touching operations. His five-part formula builds names that say why now, who it is for, what they get, how long, and what kind of package it is.

## How to apply it
Use three to five of these, in whatever order sounds punchiest:
- M, Magnetic reason why: why you are making the offer or why respond now (free, a discount, a season, grand opening, new management, an anniversary).
- A, Announce the avatar: call out exactly who it is for; for local business, go hyper-local (a neighborhood, not the city).
- G, Give them a goal: the specific, tangible dream outcome.
- I, Indicate a time interval: how long to results (days, weeks, months).
- C, Complete with a container word: challenge, blueprint, bootcamp, intensive, accelerator, sprint, reset and so on, signaling a bundled system rather than a commodity.
Rhyme or alliteration help but are optional. Name bonuses and sub-items the same way. Write several, run two or three, keep the winner as the control, and test new names against it.

When results fade, change things in this order: ad creative, then body copy, then the headline/wrapper, then the offer's duration, then the free/discount enhancer, and only last the monetization structure. Lighter changes first; the lower items create operational drag.

## When to reach for it
- A good offer gets low response or clicks.
- Local campaigns decline because you have reached the whole area.
- You feel the urge to rebuild the business when only the ad is tired.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Lazy, generic names ("Float Tank Session" versus a named stress-release challenge).
- Pairing a quantified claim with a time frame on ad platforms that ban implied guarantees.
- Changing a monetized offer out of boredom.
- Forcing rhymes.

## The math
On most platforms about $20 reaches 1,000 people, so a local area of 200,000 costs about $10,000 to reach once, which is why local offers fatigue faster. He reports good naming lifting response 2-10x on the same offer.
