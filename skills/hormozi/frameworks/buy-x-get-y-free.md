# Buy X Get Y Free
slug: buy-x-get-y-free · category: money-model · aliases: BOGO, buy one get two free, Boot Factory offer, buy 6 months get 6 free

## What it is
An attraction offer that reframes a price or discount so that buying something earns extra items or time for free. "Free" pulls more attention than "33% off," and bundling lets you sell more units or longer terms, paid up front. Hormozi learned it from a Nashville boot store that charged three times the single-pair price and then "gave away" two more pairs.

## How to apply it
1. Choose something it makes sense to buy more of, or to access for longer.
2. Decide whether you are only **reframing** (same total as buying the items separately) or also **discounting** (a lower price per unit).
3. Give away more free things than paid things: "buy 1 get 2" beats "buy 2 get 1."
4. The free items don't have to match the paid ones. Several cheap free items (three pairs of socks) can beat one expensive one.
5. For services, sell time: buy 6 months, get 12 free. For recurring businesses, have the prepaid block roll into month-to-month billing.
6. Keep making offers to people who prepaid. They are your heaviest spenders.

## When to reach for it
You rely on seasonal "sales," customers usually buy only once (make that one purchase big), payback on a service takes too long, or a recurring business needs a quick cash injection.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Giving value away without first actually raising the base price. Do raise it; some people will still buy at full price.
- Spending prepaid cash you need to deliver the full term. Budget for fulfillment.
- Selling a bulk prepay to your whole recurring base at once. Cap it at about 10% of customers so monthly cash flow holds up.

## The math
- Three $10 shirts for $30, sold as "one shirt for $30, get two free," is the same revenue with a more compelling frame. With a discount, $20 for three becomes "buy one at $20, get two free" (pp. 59–60).
- Three ways to sell 18 months at $1,800: 12 paid months plus 6 free, 9 paid plus 9 free, or 6 paid plus 12 free. The last is most attractive because it includes the most free months (p. 61).
- If typical customers stay 3 months, "buy 2 get 2" keeps them 4 (p. 64).
