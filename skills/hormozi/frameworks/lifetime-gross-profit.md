# Lifetime Gross Profit (LTV / LTGP)
slug: lifetime-gross-profit · category: pricing · aliases: LTV, LTGP, customer lifetime value, CLV, lifetime value

## What it is
Lifetime value is the **gross profit** (not revenue) a customer brings in over their whole relationship with you: everything they pay, minus what it costs to deliver it. Hormozi treats it as the fuel for advertising. Attention auctions keep getting more expensive, and whoever makes a customer worth the most can outbid everyone else for that customer. Acquisition cost can only fall to zero, while value per customer has no ceiling.

## How to apply it
1. **Gross profit per unit or per month.** Price minus direct delivery cost (materials, shipping, the labor that serves the client). Work it out per product and for the whole business. Some time-consuming products turn out to earn little.
2. **Lifetime purchases.**
   - Transactional business: export customer data, count transactions per customer, and average them. This is always an estimate, and it grows as the business ages because old customers come back.
   - Recurring business: measure **churn**, the share of customers at the start of a period who have left by the end. New sign-ups during the period don't change it.
3. **Combine.** Transactional: gross profit × average transactions. Recurring: monthly gross profit ÷ monthly churn.
4. Use the number as the baseline, then raise it with [[crazy-eight]] levers and compare it with acquisition cost.

## When to reach for it
Before setting ad budgets, pricing, or choosing which product to push. When a competitor can outspend you. When evaluating a business to buy (he prefers ones that acquire well but monetize poorly).

## Mistakes he warns about
- Using revenue or net profit instead of gross profit.
- Counting new customers in the churn calculation.
- Not knowing the number at all. He calls money math the skill rich people have.

## The math
- Product: $100 widget with $20 delivery cost gives $80 gross profit. At 4 lifetime purchases that is $320 LTGP (the playbook prints $360, but $80 × 4 = $320).
- Service: one rep costing $6,000/month serves 10 clients at $3,000/month, so $30,000 revenue and 80% margin, or $2,400 gross profit per client per month. At 5% monthly churn, $2,400 ÷ 0.05 = $48,000 LTGP.
- Churn: 100 customers at the start, 95 of them remaining, means 5% churn.
- Halving churn doubles LTV (10% → 5%, Price ÷ Churn).
