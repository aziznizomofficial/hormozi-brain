# Ignorance Tax
slug: ignorance-tax · category: mindset · aliases: time tax of ignorance, ignorance debt

## What it is
A way to think about the opportunity cost of missing knowledge. Hormozi describes it as the gap between current earnings and what someone could earn with the necessary skills and knowledge. That cost continues while the knowledge is missing. Education, experience, and useful failed attempts can reduce the gap. He also uses ignorance debt for the difference between current behavior and the behavior that would best advance a goal.

## How to apply it
1. Identify what you do not yet know how to do to reach the desired income or goal.
2. Treat acquiring that knowledge as part of the work. Consider spending on education as a way to reduce the ongoing cost of not knowing.
3. Test and iterate. Use failed attempts to learn what to change and reduce the remaining knowledge gap.
4. Learn from people with stronger relevant capabilities, even when ego or personal dislike makes that uncomfortable.
5. When starting out, evaluate work partly by the education it provides. Trading time for money can also build the skills needed for later earnings.

## When to reach for it
- You are weighing the cost of education against the cost of continuing without the knowledge.
- You mistake not knowing how to proceed for a focus problem.
- Early work pays little but provides useful experience.
- Fear of failed attempts prevents testing and learning.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Letting ego prevent learning from someone who knows more.
- Evaluating a person's entire character instead of whether they have useful knowledge to teach.
- Treating early earnings as the only return from work that also develops skills.

## The math
Annual ignorance tax = income possible with the necessary knowledge − current income.

In an example he recalls from another speaker, earning fifty thousand while lacking the knowledge to make a million represents nine hundred fifty thousand a year in ignorance tax. This expresses a hypothetical opportunity cost.
