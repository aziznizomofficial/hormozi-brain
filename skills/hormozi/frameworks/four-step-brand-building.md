# Four-Step Brand Building (Start, Grow, Pivot)
slug: four-step-brand-building · category: marketing · aliases: how to build a brand, branding roadmap, pick the avatar, associate with what they like, one-page branding checklist

## What it is
Hormozi's four-step process for building a brand from nothing, or turning a bad one into a good one. Branding is deliberately pairing your thing with things your ideal customer values, so the process begins with choosing that customer and ends with pruning the pairings based on how the audience responds. He says his own audience was built on purpose with exactly these steps.

## How to apply it
1. **Pick the ideal customer.** Narrow down so you can give more value per person. He prefers a market that is:
   - growing,
   - able to afford you,
   - easy to target with ads,
   - in pain (competitors are a good sign, because they prove people buy).
   His example: a business-law firm choosing owners of assisted-living centres.
2. **List what they like and want:** more customers and profit, a sellable business, help hiring and keeping staff, fewer lawsuits, lower taxes, and so on.
3. **Associate with those things:** make content that serves exactly those wants (videos, articles, guides, PDFs), give it away, spread it, and adjust based on how the audience reacts. Also make products they like, free and paid, and appear next to people they like. When your material makes their life better, they think of you.
4. **Optimize the associations for profit:** widen the market deliberately (up, down, adjacent, broader or narrower), keep only pairings that fit, and cut the ones your ideal customers dislike.
5. **Grow reach by advertising** the good associations, keep your promises so the product strengthens the brand, and give it about five years.

## When to reach for it
- Starting a personal or company brand with no clear identity.
- Content is attracting the wrong crowd. His own audience drifted toward "wantrepreneurs" until he refocused on business owners.
- Rebranding after a reputation hit.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Branding by accident: taking every podcast, selling to any customer, chasing trends, until no one knows what you stand for.
- Trying to serve everyone. It leaves you too many things to too many people to be profitable.
- Giving up early. Branding pays more than direct response only over long timeframes.
