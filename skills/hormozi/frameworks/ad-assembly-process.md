# Ad Assembly Process (Hooks × Meat × CTA)
slug: ad-assembly-process · category: marketing · aliases: GOATed ads, ad assembly line, 50 hooks x 3-5 meats x 1-3 CTAs, scaling past the ad wall

## What it is
Hormozi's way of mass-producing ads by building them in parts rather than one at a time. Each ad has three pieces: a **hook**, a **meat** (the body that delivers on the hook) and a **CTA**. You record many of each and combine them, which turns a single recording day into hundreds of ad variations. His diagnosis of a spend "ceiling" is that the creative has hit its limit, not the market. Better ads convert colder and larger audiences, and you only find better ads by making far more of them.

## How to apply it
1. **Block one day a week for ads.** Prep is most of the work: about 80% of prep time on hooks, 20% on meat, almost none on CTAs, because nothing else matters if people drop off at the hook.
2. **Write about 50 hooks.** Pull from, in order: your past winning ads, your own best free content, other people's ads you have saved, other people's high-view content, and finally platform ad libraries (good for ideas, but you cannot tell what actually performs there). Winning hooks often carry over between industries.
3. **Add expansion hooks** spread across awareness levels when you need new audiences (see awareness-level hooks).
4. **Record 3–5 meats** in the formats that have worked (demonstration, testimonial, education, story, faceless).
5. **Record 1–3 CTAs.**
6. **Combine and run everything,** then double down on the few that clearly outperform, reusing their hooks to make more winners.
7. Repeat weekly and keep hunting for the next winner.

## When to reach for it
- Acquisition cost jumps once daily spend passes a certain level.
- You only make a handful of new ads a month.
- Competitors copy your ads. High volume makes your winners hard to spot.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Blaming "market saturation" when the real limit is ad quality and volume.
- Making ads as whole units instead of assembling parts.
- Getting bored of winners. New buyers enter the market every day and are seeing it for the first time.

## The math
50 hooks × 3–5 meats × 1–3 CTAs = 150–750 ads per week. In his opening story, a weight-loss founder capped at roughly $5–8k/day went from about 5 ads a month to a plan of 150 a week (around 600 a month), and the company doubled in two quarters.
