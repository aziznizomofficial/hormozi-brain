# SPCL
slug: spcl · category: brand · aliases: status power credibility likeness, four pillars of influence

## What it is
A framework he uses to explain four sources of influence: status, power, credibility, and likeness. Each can independently make an audience more likely to act on a request; combining them strengthens influence. For content, the objective is to build influence rather than simply maximize views.

## How to apply it
1. Status: establish that you control resources the audience wants.
2. Power: give useful instructions that produce good outcomes when people follow them. Repeated benefits make people more likely to follow subsequent advice.
3. Credibility: provide external evidence supporting your expertise in the relevant field.
4. Likeness: help the audience recognize similarities between themselves and you.
5. Combine the four where possible. If prioritizing one, he chooses power: consistently help people get results from your instructions.

## When to reach for it
- Planning content intended to build influence with an audience.
- Assessing why an audience would trust and act on your requests.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Treating evidence of expertise in one field as automatic credibility in another.
- Using maximum views as the objective when the intended outcome is influence.
