# Churn Checklist
slug: churn-checklist · category: retention · aliases: retention checklist, decrease churn, 9-step churn checklist, four-step customer journey (activate, testimonial, refer, ascend)

## What it is
Hormozi's nine-step retention routine. Acquisition.com runs it on every portfolio company, backed by data from millions of Skool memberships. He says he doesn't know which step matters most, only that applying all of them has always cut churn. The core idea: customers stay while the value they get exceeds the price, and the most common reason they leave is **overwhelm**. Aim for high value per second, delivered through the core two or three things, and make the product easy to use.

## How to apply it
1. **Find activation points**: the behavior or result that predicts long tenure (see [[activation-points]]).
2. **Onboard** customers straight to that point. Custom beats generic, personal beats group, live beats recorded, carrots beat sticks, and some onboarding beats none. Resell the value against their goals, explain what unlocks later, set homework with a deadline, and book the next contact.
3. **Incentivize activation** with unlockables: courses, 1:1 calls, event tickets, higher-tier calls, badges. Place some just after your big churn points.
4. **Link the community.** Connect members to each other, not only to you: group events, personal introductions, a member podcast, spotlighting micro-celebrities.
5. **Correct or fire bad customers.** Praise good behavior publicly, delete bad posts with an explanation, three strikes and out, pin the best posts, and provide post categories (wins, fun, discoveries, meetups).
6. **Add annual and prepay options** (see [[annual-pricing-options]]).
7. **Run cancellation calls or videos** (see [[cancellation-save-call]]).
8. **Survey twice a year** with a keep-one question and a cut-one question about everything you provide. Between surveys, reach out 1:1 every two to three weeks using A-C-A: acknowledge, compliment, ask. Wealthier customers usually want less hand-holding.
9. **Design a customer journey** with four milestones: activate, give a testimonial, refer, ascend (buy more). Someone who just made another purchase is the least likely to churn.

## When to reach for it
Monthly churn above a few percent, a "leaky bucket" where new sales barely replace lost customers, or you're adding features but churn keeps rising.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Piling on deliverables. A newsletter owner's churn rose with every addition, and Gym Launch got shorter with each version.
- Skipping onboarding because of volume. One portfolio company's move to 1:1 onboarding raised ascensions 25%.

## The math
Reducing churn from 10% to 3% extends average customer tenure from 10 months to about 33, increasing LTV 3.3x (p. 13). Personal 1:1 outreach at intervals of two to three weeks retained more customers than making no contact (p. 29).
