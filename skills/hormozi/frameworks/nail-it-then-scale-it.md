# Nail It Then Scale It
slug: nail-it-then-scale-it · category: scaling · aliases: nail it before you scale it, nail then scale, scale zero

## What it is
A sequence for making a business repeatable before replicating it. First bring the existing operation to a performance level you are happy with. Then reach scale zero: remove the owner's operating involvement while the business performs the same or better. Replicate the model using the economics it has when it requires none of the founder's time.

## How to apply it
1. Develop the current operation until its performance reaches a level you are happy with.
2. Work toward scale zero: the owner does zero in the business while results stay the same or improve.
3. Use that owner-independent operation as the model to duplicate.
4. Repeat the actions that already work. Higher volume creates coordination and systems demands on its own.
5. As growth exposes something that breaks, fix it and return to scaling.

## When to reach for it
- You are considering another location while the current one still depends on you.
- You want a model that can be duplicated across operations.
- Growth is introducing coordination problems or exposing weaknesses in delivery.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Replicating a business before it can sustain performance without the owner.
- Adding unnecessary novelty when increasing volume already makes the business more complex.
