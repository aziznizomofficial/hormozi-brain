# Post Free Content
slug: free-content · category: content · aliases: core four #2, audience building, personal brand, puddles-ponds-lakes-oceans

## What it is
The second core-four method: one-to-many communication to a warm audience. Warm outreach means repeating yourself for every person; content lets you say it once and reach everyone. People who value it join your warm audience and share it, pulling in more. Hormozi's reframe, after years of dismissing content: posts fade, but the audience is the compounding asset. Content also lifts every other channel, since prospects check you out before buying.

## How to apply it
- Build each piece with [[hook-retain-reward]]; monetise with [[give-ask-ratio]]; grow reach with [[depth-vs-width]].
- Post much more than feels necessary. The paid advice he got amounted to "do more"; after roughly tenfold output his audience grew about ten times faster (pp. 112–113).
- His lessons: talk about "how I" and "my favourite ways", not "how to" and "the best way"; repeat yourself, since many followers miss any given post; start in a small niche and place (a puddle) and widen over time; keep a labelled "greatest hits" list for salespeople to send prospects; remember free content also retains paying customers; attention spans are not shorter, standards are higher; post manually rather than pre-scheduling so you feel the pressure to make it good.
- Keep doing warm outreach alongside content; content enlarges the list you reach out to.

## When to reach for it
- Warm outreach is running out of people.
- Paid ads are underperforming for no obvious reason (see below).

## Mistakes he warns about
- Ego: dismissing other people's results as luck instead of studying them.
- Crediting only paid ads. When his ads declined in 2020, the cause was that he had stopped making niche content (pp. 141–142).
- Content's trade-offs: harder to personalise, more competition, and winners get copied, so keep innovating.

## The math
Surveyed clients: before booking a call, 78% had watched or read two or more long-form pieces (p. 142). Track monthly: total followers and reach (size) and month-over-month growth rate, e.g. 1,000 to 1,500 followers is 50% growth. Fix input cadence first so output numbers mean something; his podcast took about five years of twice-weekly episodes to reach a US business top 10.
