# Four Types of Leverage
slug: four-types-of-leverage · category: scaling · aliases: four cs of leverage, four forms of leverage, four sources of leverage

## What it is
Hormozi's adaptation of Naval Ravikant's leverage categories: collaboration, capital, code, and content. Leverage increases the output obtained from a given amount of input. People contribute labor, capital puts money to work, code automates work, and content multiplies attention. Labor and outside investment require other people's agreement; code and content can be initiated independently and replicated without securing agreement for each copy.

## How to apply it
1. Identify the business constraint. Choose leverage that helps resolve it.
2. Collaboration: use other people's time to free capacity for more valuable work. His example is paying someone $10 an hour for tasks while using the freed time to earn $50 an hour selling on the phone.
3. Capital: consider how other people's money could expand what the business can accomplish.
4. Code: use software to automate work and return time.
5. Content: use media to reach more people from the same underlying work. One example is distributing a podcast on YouTube.
6. Examine inputs and outputs to find where more leverage is possible. Deepen one form or combine several where they increase returns.

## When to reach for it
- You want to increase output without a matching increase in personal effort.
- You are comparing opportunities and need to assess how much leverage each permits.
- You have identified a constraint and need additional labor, money, automation, or audience attention to address it.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Automating more without knowing the business constraint; additional automation does not necessarily produce more money.
- Assuming all four types are required. Developing one type extensively can be enough.
