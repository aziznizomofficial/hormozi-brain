# Giveaway Offer
slug: giveaway-offer · category: money-model · aliases: Giveaways, scholarship offer, sweepstakes, partial scholarship, "many will enter…many will win" (p. 42)

## What it is
An attraction offer that advertises a chance to win a big, valuable prize in exchange for contact details and qualifying answers. One person wins the grand prize. Everyone else is told they qualified for a smaller prize: a discount, credit or voucher toward the same core offer. The entrants have already shown they want your most expensive product.

## How to apply it
1. **Grand prize:** make it the thing you want everyone to buy and put a real retail value on it, because that value becomes the price anchor.
2. **Promotional offer:** the participation trophy of a scholarship, gift card, voucher or dollars off the core offer.
3. **Entry:** get permission to follow up, ask eligibility questions (why they should be chosen, what their goal is), and optionally require qualifying actions such as posting, attending or joining a group.
4. **Deadline:** promote for 3–7 days with a daily countdown carrying value and proof.
5. **Announce** the winner publicly, then privately contact everyone else to book a call to claim their prize, with a second deadline (about 7 days).
6. On the call, present the discounted grand-prize offer first. If they decline, apply the same percentage discount to another product.

## When to reach for it
You need a surge of qualified leads fast, have a high-ticket core offer, and can service a burst of sales calls.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Giveaways are heavily regulated and can become an illegal lottery. Get legal advice. The grand prize must actually be awarded, and the rules should say more than one person can win.
- A weak prize. If nobody enters, the prize wasn't grand enough (event tickets flopped; a $50k equipment bundle worked).
- Letting in more entrants than you can call within about 7 days.
- No downsell for people who can't afford the promotional offer.
- The more work it takes to enter, the fewer (but better) entrants you get. Find the balance.

## The math
He sets the core-offer discount at roughly 10–30% of gross margin. A $5,000-value prize retailing at $2,000, offered at $1,800, is only 10% off retail, but it reads as a 64% gap between cost and value (p. 46). Two prizes: if someone you referred wins, you win too, which roughly doubles leads (p. 47).
