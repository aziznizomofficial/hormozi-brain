# Premium Offers (the offer on its own)
slug: premium-offers · category: pricing · aliases: premium promotions, high-ticket front end, apply and book a call, infinite upside pricing

## What it is
Presenting your Grand Slam Offer with no free or discount wrapper: ask the client to buy whatever gives them the best possible outcome. Hormozi's insight came from a story about a $50k burger: at earnings of $25k per hour, it costs two hours of work, just as a $13.50 meal does at an hourly wage of $6.75. Price cannot fall below zero but has no upper limit. Premium offers therefore support a much larger traffic budget per customer and require far less volume. They differ from free or discount offers without being inherently better or worse.

## How to apply it
- Requires two things: something genuinely very valuable, and a sales process that demonstrates that value.
- Load the offer with bonuses, creative guarantees and premium support so it still feels like a steal.
- Know the avatar intimately and write specific copy (their real daily grind, not generic "hard work").
- Can stand alone, or be the second offer after a free one, which works only if the first offer proved value.
- Starting from nothing? He wouldn't lead with premium. Start free or discounted, prove results, then restructure.
- After the free phase, his first premium front end asked owners to apply and schedule a qualification call; the preceding copy filtered for owners who understood the high price.

**Pros:** simplest math and fewest moving parts; fewer, higher-quality customers so less operational drag; no incentives needed and clear intentions, so strong lead quality.
**Cons:** priciest leads and slower break-even; needs strong copywriting and deep avatar knowledge; needs real sales skill; least efficient way to capture a market; demands an extremely compelling offer.

## When to reach for it
You have a proven offer and sales process, cash to learn, and prefer profit per client over market share. Existing businesses can often raise prices 3–5x with this structure without changing the service.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Leading with premium before you have proof, a sales process, or cash to burn while learning.
- Thin offers at high prices.

## The math
Business A: $100 sale, $50 profit, needs 190 sales to earn $9,500. Offer those same 190 people a $10,000 product ($9,500 profit) and close about 5%: 9.5 sales = $90,250, about 9.5x as profitable with a tenth of the clients.
