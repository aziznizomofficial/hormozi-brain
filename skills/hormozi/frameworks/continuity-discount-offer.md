# Continuity Discount Offer
slug: continuity-discount-offer · category: money-model · aliases: discount continuity, free-but-when, X months free with commitment, lifetime discount at churn point

## What it is
Give free time (or a discount) to customers who commit to a recurring term today. It is Buy X Get Y Free adapted for continuity. Hormozi's neighbor built and sold a trash-hauling company by offering apartment complexes a free first year in exchange for five paid years. The two decisions that matter are **where the discount is applied** and **what happens when someone cancels**.

## How to apply it
1. Choose where the discount lands:
   - **Up front:** free time first, then the paid term. This converts best but delays cash, and only suits industries that enforce contracts or hold collateral. Avoid it if churn is high.
   - **At the end:** free time is earned by making every payment on time. Fewer sign-ups, lower churn.
   - **Spread across the term:** three free months on a $200/month year becomes $50 off every month. Optionally, keep the discount for life if they pay on time.
   - **After the first 1–2 payments** (first-and-last month, or an activation fee), which also confirms the card works.
2. Extend the term instead of eating into it: 12 paid months plus 3 free, not 9 plus 3. You can feature-downsell to a shorter term later.
3. Deliver the discount as a physical gift card they can use later or pass to a friend. Some are never redeemed, and gifted ones bring referrals.
4. Offer a lifetime rate unlocked just past your most common drop-off month.
5. Decide the cancellation policy in advance. His preference: cancelling means repaying the discount received, which puts them back on month-to-month pricing.
6. Make cancelling easy to find, and require an exit conversation (waive the fee if they come in).

## When to reach for it
Rentals, internet, pool, lawn, gyms and any recurring service where a commitment makes the economics work.

## Mistakes he warns about
Front-loading in high-churn businesses. Hiding the cancellation route, which pushes complaints into public reviews. Light terms bring more sign-ups but more churn, and harsh terms do the reverse.

## The math
Spread example: $600 of discount ÷ 12 = $50/month (p. 156). A rice seller charged full price, 5% off on subscription, or 15% off for staying five months (p. 158). Billing cadence and fee tactics are on [[four-week-billing]].
