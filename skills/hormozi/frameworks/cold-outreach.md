# Cold Outreach
slug: cold-outreach · category: leads · aliases: core four #3, cold calling, cold email, cold DMs, big fast value

## What it is
The third core-four method: one-to-one contact with strangers. It is warm outreach without the limit of who knows you, and it lets you choose exactly who you target instead of accepting whoever finds you. The difference is trust, which creates three problems solved in order: you have no way to reach them (build a list), they ignore you (say the right thing), and they are not interested yet (volume and follow-up).

## How to apply it
1. List: test samples from scraping software first, then list brokers, then hand-build lists from groups and communities. Hand-built lists are slowest but freshest, since fewer others have contacted those people.
2. Message: personalise with one to three facts a friend would know, compliment them, and make the cold message feel warm. Then offer substantial value quickly: provide something free that usually carries a price. Keep scripts short and send the first 100 calls or 10,000 emails before tweaking.
3. Volume: automate delivery (recorded voicemails, templates) and distribution (dialers, blasts); spend 10–20% of effort testing new tools. Personalise more when the audience is small.
4. Follow up many times through many channels, using a missed email as the reason to call. Expect two to three conversations before a high-ticket sale. After a full pass, wait three to six months and run the list again, since timing and circumstances change.

## When to reach for it
- You want faster or more predictable lead flow than warm methods give.
- Its advantages: one message to perfect, invisible to competitors, reliable, few platform rule changes, fewer ad-policy limits, no spokesperson needed (more sellable), hard to copy.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Weak lead magnets: swapping a disguised sales call for real free service tripled take rates (p. 170).
- Underestimating volume and time: he expected twelve weeks; it took almost a year.
- Not tracking every metric; his two failed attempts had poor tracking.

## The math
Benchmarks: calls, 100 dials a day, 20% pick up, 25% of those accept (the book tallies about four engaged leads, roughly one per hour of dialling); email, aim for about 3% engaged (30% open times 10% reply); personal video DMs, about 20% reply. Target at least 3x lifetime profit versus acquisition cost. Cost example: reps at $15/hour plus $50 per show, $3,600 profit per sale, 33% close, about $360 to acquire, a 10:1 return. Reverse-engineer: 1 customer per 100 emails means 10,000 emails for 100 customers.
