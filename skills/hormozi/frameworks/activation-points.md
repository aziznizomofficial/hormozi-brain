# Activation Points
slug: activation-points · category: retention · aliases: activation point, retention point, leading indicator of retention, time to value, unlockables, usage churn

## What it is
An activation point is the action a customer takes, or the result they get, that predicts they'll stay much longer than people who don't. Hormozi calls finding it the top retention priority, because once you know it you can steer every new customer toward it. At Skool, hosts who get their first three group members stay far longer, so Skool helps hosts get members. At Gym Launch, clients who earned back their investment within 30 days stayed, so the Fast Cash Play was created to make that happen for everyone.

## How to apply it
1. Pull the data on customers who churned, keep those who stayed 3+ months (any cutoff works), sort them by spend, and take the top 20%.
2. Study them: demographics, psychographics, income, business size, how they used the product and how you treated them. Survey them if you're missing data.
3. List the shared factors, narrow to about five candidates, and test which ones actually move retention.
4. Update **messaging** to attract more customers like your best ones, and update **onboarding** to drive every new customer to the activation point as fast as possible.
5. Reward activation with unlockables, and put extra perks just past your common churn month (if most leave after month 3, unlock something in month 4).
6. Watch for **usage churn**: customers still paying but no longer using the product. Contact them the moment usage drops.
7. Re-test every 6–12 months.

## When to reach for it
Churn is high and you don't know why, onboarding is generic, or ads bring in customers who don't last.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Waiting and hoping customers reach the milestone on their own instead of helping them get there.
- Serving everyone. Who you serve matters more than what you sell. Price for the best customers and ignore the rest.

## The math
- Gym Launch vs a competitor with similar acquisition costs: Hormozi's pre-qualified clients were worth $42,000 in year one against the competitor's $5,000, for about 70x the profit (p. 18).
- Driving clients to recoup their investment in 30 days took churn from 8% to 3% within 6 months (p. 19).
- A PR agency that served only its best-fit avatar grew 10x in 24 months (p. 18).
- Typical activation points: first leads (B2B service), first login to the dashboard (software), first use of a consumable (physical product) (p. 19).
