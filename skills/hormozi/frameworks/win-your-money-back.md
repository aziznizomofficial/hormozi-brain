# Win Your Money Back
slug: win-your-money-back · category: money-model · aliases: WYMB, pay-to-play challenge, money-back challenge, attraction offer #1

## What it is
An attraction offer requiring payment upfront, with a refund in cash or store credit earned by reaching your specified goal and following your prescribed path. It was Hormozi's first Grand Slam Offer at Gym Launch and the one that grew his gyms most. The customer can qualify by results, by completing actions, or both.

## How to apply it
1. Pick the criteria: **results** (bets on their ability), **actions** such as attendance, logging, photos and homework (bets on following directions), or **both**.
2. Make criteria (a) easy to track, ideally with tools they already use, (b) the things your best customers do anyway, and (c) promotional: posts, tags, reviews, referrals.
3. Make check-in meetings mandatory for qualifying. Each one is a natural moment to make the next offer (supplements at orientation, membership at progress and final reviews).
4. Pay out as store credit and roll it into something that costs more than the credit, spread across a longer term (see rollover upsell).
5. About halfway through, tell everyone privately that you'll credit them whether or not they hit the short-term goal. At the end, treat people who fell short as winners too: they finished, so credit the deposit toward staying.

## When to reach for it
Businesses where results depend on customer effort over time (fitness, coaching, new skills, starting a business). Also useful for pulling back former customers.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Running it when your refund rate is already above about 5%. Fix the product first.
- Running it if paying refunds will upset you. Roughly 10% of gym customers asked for their money back.
- Handing credit back as free months up front. People who pay nothing tend to drop off.
- Having nothing to sell next. The profit comes from the people who succeed and then buy the next thing, not from people who fail.

## The math
A $600 credit on a $200/month service becomes $50 off per month for 12 months, so the customer pays $150/month instead of receiving three free months (pp. 37–38). Store credit pulled in about as many buyers as cash back did in his tests.
