# Warm Outreach
slug: warm-outreach · category: leads · aliases: warm reach outs, first five clients, core four #1, nine-word email

## What it is
The first of the core four: one-to-one contact with people who already know you (phone, email and social contacts, past customers). Hormozi calls it the cheapest, easiest, most reliable lead source, and the one most businesses skip. Few leads per hour, since every message is personal, but it always works and later scales with automation and staff.

## How to apply it
His ten steps, paraphrased:
1. Build your list from every phone, inbox and social account; most people find their first thousand contacts.
2. Start on the platform with the most contacts.
3. Open with a personal, no-ask check-in based on something real about them.
4. Contact 100 people a day, up to three touches (daily; weekly for mail) or until they reply.
5. When they reply, keep it human with [[aca-framework]].
6. After a few exchanges, ask whether they know anyone who wants your result. Build it from the value equation (raise dream outcome and likelihood, cut time delay and effort) and cite results from people like them.
7. Make it free for the first five in return for using it, giving feedback and leaving an honest review.
8. Move to the next platform and repeat.
9. Once referrals start, charge: 80% off for the next five, then 60%, 40%, raising steadily (he suggests +20% every five).
10. Keep the list warm with value, then send a short "are you still looking to [desire]?" email (credited to Dean Jackson) and prioritise the replies.

## When to reach for it
- Launching any new product or service with zero clients.
- Advanced businesses: re-engaging old customers or launching a new line.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Pushing to buy. Asking "who do you know" lets them volunteer interest without pressure.
- If people refuse it free, your hidden costs (time, effort, sacrifice) or credibility are the problem; ask what would make it worth it.
- Doing too little: he prescribes about four hours a day, first thing.

## The math
Benchmarks: about 1 in 5 contacts reply; about 1 in 5 of those take the free offer; about 1 in 4 of those later pay, so roughly one customer per 100 reach outs. At 500 reach outs a week and a $400 offer, that is about $104,000 a year. Limits: your time and the number of people who know you.
