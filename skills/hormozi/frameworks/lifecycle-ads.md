# Lifecycle Ads (Before / During / After Clips)
slug: lifecycle-ads · category: marketing · aliases: life cycle ads, customer journey ads, success interviews, upon-delivery ads, reaction ads, ribbon-cutting moments

## What it is
A Marketing Machine node that builds ads from real moments across one customer's journey. You record every customer interaction, then cut together a *before* moment (from the sales call), a *during* moment (from support calls) and an *after* moment (from an upsell, milestone, award or testimonial call). Instead of a customer describing their hard times, the viewer sees them at their low point and then at their win. For businesses with a physical "reveal," the equivalent is the **upon-delivery ad**: capture the raw reaction when the customer first sees the result.

## How to apply it
1. **Legal:** add consent and permission language to every customer contract. Still ask before running anyone's story, and respect a no.
2. **IT:** record all sales, onboarding, support, ascension and milestone calls. Buy enough storage and turn off auto-delete.
3. **Everyone:** put the client's name in every calendar event so calls can be found later.
4. **Sales:** send marketing a weekly list of happy customers.
5. **Marketing:** search the calendar for each name, check dates match, then clip the before, during and after moments.
6. **Label the clips consistently** so anyone can pull them in seconds, then run them.
7. **Upon-delivery version:** film the first look at a remodelled kitchen, new veneers, a weigh-in or a "you got the job" call. Use the raw reaction as the first 3–5 seconds (the hook), make an ad from each one, and cut highlight reels of reactions only.

## When to reach for it
- A remote or service business where every interaction already happens on video or phone.
- You want proof that shows the transformation rather than claims it.
- Businesses with dramatic delivery moments that go uncaptured.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Letting big moments pass without a camera. Many businesses have them and never capture them.
- Underestimating the editing. Lifecycle ads take the most editing work of any node, so he places them last in the rollout order even though he loves them.
