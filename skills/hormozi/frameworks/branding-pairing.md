# Branding as Pairing (Reach, Influence, Direction)
slug: branding-pairing · category: marketing · aliases: what branding is, branded cow, advertising vs branding, good/bad/no branding, brand metrics, bad publicity, polarizing brands

## What it is
Hormozi's working definition: branding means pairing your business with other things (people, values, outcomes, experiences) so that seeing your name changes what people do. **Good branding** pairs you with things most of your ideal audience likes. **Bad branding** pairs you with things they dislike. **No branding** leaves their behaviour unchanged, so you are effectively invisible. Advertising and branding are separate jobs. Advertising tells people your thing exists (the cow belongs to *someone*). Branding tells them what it means that it is *yours*. You can be good at one and bad at the other.

## How to apply it
1. **Measure advertising by reach:** how many of the people you want know about you.
2. **Measure branding by influence and direction.** Influence is how likely seeing you is to change behaviour. Direction is whether it moves people towards you or away. The same brand can pull one audience and repel another (politicians are his example).
3. **Map your position:** high or low reach × high or low influence × towards or away gives eight positions. Any brand sits in different positions for different groups.
4. **Judge with money:** good branding means your ideal audience likes you *and buys*. Bad branding means they dislike you and do not.
5. **Choose pairings deliberately.** Customers are pairing you with everything you say, do and appear next to anyway.

## When to reach for it
- Great ads that still convert poorly, or a big reach with little pull.
- You are deciding on a sponsorship, spokesperson, cause or controversial post.
- The team argues about brand as "vibes" rather than behaviour.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Using definitions of brand that describe feelings but give you nothing to *do*. His test of learning is the same situation producing new behaviour.
- The Pepsi/Kendall Jenner protest ad: huge reach, but a pairing much of the audience disliked, and it was pulled within days. Good advertising, bad branding.
- "There's no such thing as bad publicity" mixes up reach and branding. Any attention beats none, but good beats bad.
- Deliberately trying to be polarizing. Selling polarizes people anyway. Strong brands can be broadly liked.
