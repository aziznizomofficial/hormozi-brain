# Rollover Upsell
slug: rollover-upsell · category: money-model · aliases: roll it forward, credit toward the next thing, winback campaign, gift card play

## What it is
Credit some or all of what a customer has already paid (to you, or even to a competitor) toward a bigger next purchase. The credit feels like found money, so far more people accept. Hormozi calls it the upsell he uses most. It fixed his early gyms, where winners of Win Your Money Back used their credit for three free months and then left. His friend instead spread the credit as $50 off per month across a 12-month membership, so members kept paying from day one.

## How to apply it
Decide **who**, **what** and **how**:
1. **Who:** lapsed customers (6+ months since last purchase), upset customers (as an alternative to a refund), competitors' unhappy customers (find them in negative reviews), and current customers right after they buy.
2. **What:** more of the same, a better version, or something new, always priced above the credit.
3. **How:** credit all or part of it, applied up front or spread over the term.
4. Make it a one-time offer, available only at that moment, to create urgency.
5. **Gift card play:** sell $200 gift cards for $20, at most two per customer, usable only by someone else. When a friend redeems one, roll the $200 into an offer worth $1,000 or more.

## When to reach for it
Customers churn right after their first block ends, you face refund requests, you have an old customer list going unused, or competitors are frustrating their buyers.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Refunding before offering a rollover or a do-over.
- Handing credit back as free time instead of reducing ongoing payments.
- Pricing the next offer so close to the credit that you lose money.
- Assuming you have to roll over the full amount. Test how much credit it takes.

## The math
- Price the next offer at 4x the credit or more, so even a full rollover is at most a 25% discount (p. 105).
- A $600 purchase becomes $50/month off a 12-month plan (p. 104).
- Winback: personal videos to 200 past customers offering $4,000 of credit got about 20% to accept, around $1.9M in added annual revenue from one day of recording (p. 104).
