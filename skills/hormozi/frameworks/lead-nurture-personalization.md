# Lead Nurture Personalization
slug: lead-nurture-personalization · category: leads · aliases: personalization pillar, preferred communication method, segment your messaging, matched proof, blue texts, local area codes

## What it is
The third pillar of lead nurture: make every touch useful, relevant and pleasant for that particular lead. The core logic is to find out what the lead wants, line it up for them, and tell them they get it by showing up. That lowers their risk of wasting time and raises what they lose by skipping. Personalization drives replies, and in his data, leads who reply are the ones who show.

## How to apply it
Hormozi lists six tactics. Qualifying and routing, and show-up incentives, have their own cards.
1. **Preferred channel:** open on every channel at once (call, text, DM, email) as if you really need to reach them. Then carry on wherever they answer first, and move to richer media (phone, video) later.
2. **Qualify:** use applications and demographic data. Remove leads who are clearly a poor fit from the calendar to free time for likely buyers.
3. **Best leads to best closers:** score and route.
4. **Segment messaging.** *Easy version:* spend five minutes researching a good lead (profile, website, recent activity) and open with something specific about them. *Hard version:* write separate text and email sequences for each lead category, such as revenue band or goal size, with pains and case studies to match.
5. **Incentivize showing up:** push (gift) or pull (A/B choice).
6. **Matched proof:** send testimonials from people as close to the lead as possible (age, situation, goal). Send them after booking, between first and second calls, and to ghosts every 90 days, showing results from people who started around when they went quiet. Stay compliant with local rules.

Deliverability tips: text from real phones, since iMessage-style "blue" messages get more replies than messages that look automated, and use local-area-code calling technology to raise pick-up rates.

## When to reach for it
- Low reply rates to templated follow-ups.
- Leads go silent between booking and the appointment.
- One offer serves several distinct segments.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Reaching out once, on one platform, and going through the motions.
- Sending everyone the same template regardless of what they told you.

## The math
He cites HubSpot improving email marketing ROI 7x after segmenting its list.
