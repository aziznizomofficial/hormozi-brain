# Billing Frequency (28-Day Cycles and Annual Prepay)
slug: billing-frequency · category: pricing · aliases: bill less frequently, 13 billing cycles, 28-day billing, annual billing, lookback window of value, display small bill long

## What it is
Billing every four weeks instead of monthly quietly adds a thirteenth payment each year. Billing less often (quarterly, annually) cuts churn because every charge is a moment to reconsider, and customers judge you only on the value delivered since their last payment, which he calls the lookback window. How you display a price and how you bill it can differ.

## How to apply it
1. Switch monthly plans to every 28 days (or 12 weeks). Apply this to new contracts now; choose and clearly communicate a changeover date for existing contracts.
2. Display the smallest unit (per week or per day) but bill the longest practical cycle; he avoids weekly and biweekly billing because of pause requests.
3. Offer prepay options. On the phone, anchor with the full-year total first, offer a roughly 17% prepay discount for the year, then a smaller discount (about 8%) for a quarter, then plain monthly.
4. To keep conversion high, sell a short 6-12 week start, then upsell a full year once trust is built.

## When to reach for it
- Monthly subscriptions with steady cancellations.
- Clients cancel after a slow month despite a great first month.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Billing more often than you deliver visible value.
- Quoting the discounted price first, which makes monthly look like a penalty instead of prepay looking like a reward.

## The math
- 13 vs 12 cycles = +8.3% revenue; at 20% net margin that moves margin to about 26% and profit up about 41%. Every 21 days at $99 = $1,716/yr vs $1,200 (+43%).
- Profitwell data he cites: with annual billing, monthly churn is about 2% and LTV about $5,000 at $100/mo; quarterly billing has 5% monthly churn and $2,000 LTV; monthly billing has 10.7% monthly churn and $935 LTV.
- With a 16% annual discount, 10-15% choose annual on a page, about 30% if it is the default, 35-40% by phone. If 30% prepay $1,000, the average first payment rises from $100 to $370.
- Lookback example: a client paying $5,000/mo who made $60,000 in month one but $0 in month two will likely cancel.
