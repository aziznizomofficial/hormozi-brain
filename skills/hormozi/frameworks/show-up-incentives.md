# Show-Up Incentives (Push Gift vs A/B Pull)
slug: show-up-incentives · category: leads · aliases: gift card incentive, A/B incentive, reciprocity gift, bribe to show, black or pink shirt

## What it is
A personalization tactic for raising show rates. You give the lead a concrete reason to attend that is already set up for them. It came from an ALAN beta tester whose show rate beat everyone else's. He was texting leads outside the system to ask which colour of free shirt they wanted, and almost everyone who picked a colour showed up. Hormozi sorts these incentives into two types. Both act as a small social contract.

## How to apply it
1. **Push incentive (a gift):** send something before the appointment, whether or not they attend. For example, a small coffee gift card "so you're caffeinated," or free products set aside at a salon or clinic. It works through reciprocity, even when people can see what you are doing, and it tends to lift close rates too. Keep the amount modest but not insulting. A gift has no strings.
2. **Pull incentive (an A/B choice):** ask the lead to choose between two options they will receive when they arrive. The question assumes they will come, and it shows you are spending something to prepare. Almost anything can be framed as A or B: quantity, flavour, colour, size, start date, time slot, format, delivery speed, staff member, contact method.
3. **Pull choices have three requirements:** the person wants the item, its connection to your eventual offer is obvious, and preparing it visibly costs you something real.
4. Deliver the ask on the first fast contact call, right after booking.

## When to reach for it
- Low show rates on free consults, trials or first visits.
- Brick-and-mortar or service businesses with cheap-to-give extras.
- Leads book but feel little at stake.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Turning the gift into a hidden condition. A push gift is theirs regardless.
- Offering an A/B item that has nothing to do with your offer, or one that obviously cost you nothing.

## The math
None. A gift of a few dollars, such as the $5 coffee card in his example, is enough.
