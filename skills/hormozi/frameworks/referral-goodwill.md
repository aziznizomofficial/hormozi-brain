# Goodwill: Six Ways to Earn More Referrals
slug: referral-goodwill · category: retention · aliases: price vs value gap, customer surplus, remarkable product, one customer thought experiment, six value levers

## What it is
The first reason businesses get few referrals: the product is not as good as the owner thinks. In Hormozi's model, price is the amount paid and value is the benefit received; the difference is **goodwill** (economists' customer surplus). Lots of goodwill produces word of mouth. Cutting price builds it only temporarily and costs margin, so the real lever is giving more value. His six ways map onto the parts of an ad.

## How to apply it
1. **Callout → sell better customers.** Find what your lowest-churn, most successful customers share and target only them. A PR portfolio company refocused on a niche that was just 15% of its business; it broke a multi-year plateau, ad costs fell, and referrals followed.
2. **Dream outcome → set better expectations.** You set expectations, so you can beat them. Trim promises gradually until close rates start to dip, then stop.
3. **Likelihood → get more people better results.** Survey for best results, interview those customers, find shared actions, force new customers to repeat them, measure, then tie guarantee conditions to those actions. At Gym Launch, LTGP was triple for owners who both advertised and closed a sale within their initial seven days.
4. **Time delay → faster, more frequent wins.** Split deliverables into smaller pieces, send frequent updates, pack wins into the first 48 hours after purchase, always say when they will hear from you next (book the next meeting from the current one), and pad timelines by 50% so you deliver early.
5. **Effort and sacrifice → keep improving the product.** Monthly loop: find the most common complaint, borrow fixes from customers who succeeded anyway, pilot with struggling customers, roll out if solved, move to the next.
6. **CTA → tell them what to buy next.** Satisfy their urge to buy or a competitor will. Make each next offer more compelling than the first.

## When to reach for it
Referral rate below churn; owners insisting "everyone loves us, we just need exposure."

## Mistakes he warns about
- Overpromising to close; fulfilment becomes a nightmare.
- Obsessing over the front-end offer and neglecting the back end.
- Assuming customers will forgive a miss. They won't; plan for it.

## The math
Thought experiment: all customers gone but one, and every future customer must come from them. How would you treat that person? His distinction: price measures the payment you collect; value measures the benefit the customer receives. (p. 265)
