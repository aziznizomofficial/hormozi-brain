# Automatic Continuity (Continued Access)
slug: automatic-continuity · category: money-model · aliases: continued access, roll into a smaller monthly, back-end continuity, storage for services

## What it is
Instead of selling lifetime access or letting customers simply finish and leave, you sell a fixed term and have it roll automatically, with upfront agreement, into a much cheaper, low-cost-to-deliver version afterwards. Buyers accept it easily because it is far off and small compared with what they already paid, and after the term they keep paying to avoid losing access. Over time you build a pool of low-ticket customers you can resell.

## How to apply it
1. List every front-end product or service you sell.
2. Find a feature customers value that costs little to keep providing: continued access, support, priority support, community, price protection, insurance or warranty.
3. Price it at 5-20% of the main offer.
4. Attach it to every front-end purchase, starting after the initial term (e.g., after a year or after the program ends); it also works after one-time sales.
5. Disclose it clearly at signup; make it the default.
6. Remarket more offers to this pool over time.

## When to reach for it
- Customers finish a program and disappear.
- You sell lifetime access and are stuck serving people forever for one payment.
- You want recurring profit without new fulfillment.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Hiding it; this is agreed-upon continuity, not forced or undisclosed billing.
- Selling "pay once, access forever" at all, unless it truly costs you nothing to fulfill (Price Raise Playbook).

## The math
Main service: $2,000/mo for 4 months at 70% margin = $5,600 LTV. Add continuity at $200/mo (10% of the rate), 90% margin, average 20 months, 50% of customers staying on: about +$1,800 per customer, LTV $7,400 (+32%). A seminar speaker said this line in his contract made him $750,000 in a year; in Hormozi's business it produced about $150,000/mo in profit.
