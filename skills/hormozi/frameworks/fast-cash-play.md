# Fast Cash Play
slug: fast-cash-play · category: money-model · aliases: Fast Cash offer, cash play, quarterly promotion, cash calendar, 7-day ultra-premium campaign

## What it is
A limited-time, ultra-premium offer sent only to your warmest audience (current customers, past customers in good standing, engaged leads) about once a quarter. It combines limited spots, exclusivity, a very high price and a short deadline. The leads are already acquired, and premium services carry high margins, so almost all the revenue is profit. Hormozi's early cash plays were about 15% of revenue but nearly half of profit. He first ran one for Gym Launch clients hit by surprise tax bills, and the average gym collected about $30,000 extra within days.

## How to apply it
1. **List:** existing customers, past customers and engaged leads. Leave out anyone you haven't contacted in six months or more.
2. **Offer:** one to three core components built from things that don't scale: attention (1:1 or many-on-1), personalization, convenience, status, duration, speed, white-glove experience, access, network, secrets. Prepare **three bonuses** to release one at a time. Everyone who buys gets all of them.
3. **Price:** your average transaction × 10, up to × 50. For recurring businesses, a year prepaid (monthly × 12) plus bonuses works well.
4. **Cap spots** at a number you'll definitely sell out, around 5–10% of customers.
5. **Promote for 7 days or less** with paired emails and texts: a teaser, the announcement, a recap plus a bonus, a sales update, "X spots left" plus a bonus, a final warning with the best bonus, a sold-out notice, and optionally a "someone backed out" message. For consult-based sales, open the offer immediately and book calls. For automated checkout, build anticipation and then open the cart to a rush.
6. After delivery, collect the 4 Rs (reviews, results, referrals, resells), let the list rest with value-only content, and set the next date.

## When to reach for it
An unexpected expense (taxes, repairs, legal), funding expansion, a customer base with no premium option, or simply a quarterly raise for yourself.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Running it weekly (fatigue) or yearly (money left on the table). Quarterly is the sweet spot.
- Balking at "unscalable" perks. The point is extraordinary value that justifies the price.
- Poor execution makes you seem like a pushy promoter and alienates good customers.

## The math
Example gym: 166 members paying $150 each month generate $300k in annual revenue, with $75k profit at a 25% margin. Four quarterly plays (10×$6k, 13×$5k, 8×$6k, 14×$4k) add $229k. Revenue rises 76% and net income roughly quadruples to about $281k, plus 8 new customers by its quarterly figures (pp. 18–19; the playbook's summary says 22). Aim for 90%+ gross margin on these offers.
