# Trial With Penalty
slug: trial-with-penalty · category: sales · aliases: free trial with penalty, trial downsell, skin-in-the-game trial, start-free-if-you-do-the-work

## What it is
A free trial with conditions attached. The customer gets the product free as long as they do what makes customers succeed (attend onboarding, do homework, show up to check-ins). If they skip those things, they pay fees. It mirrors Win Your Money Back, but compliance avoids charges instead of earning a refund. Hormozi uses it as a downsell after someone turns down the main offer. It changes only what they pay today, not the total.

## How to apply it
1. Pick what they get free: your bare-bones version or your Win Your Money Back program. Give more rather than less if you can afford it.
2. Reuse your Win Your Money Back criteria (activation plus self-promotion) as the conditions for avoiding fees.
3. Offer the trial last, only after a clear no.
4. **Always take a card.** Say it's simply how you've always done it. If they won't leave a card, don't start the trial.
5. **Sell staying and paying first.** Ask whether they'll stay long-term if it gets them the result. If not, there's no point in a trial.
6. **Once their card is saved, explain the fees,** and ask them to initial every fee clause.
7. Make check-ins mandatory. They are your upsell meetings.
8. Afterward: likers get longer or better plans; haters get you angrier than they are, then a better-fit offer (about half buy); non-users get the fee waived if they come in.

## When to reach for it
Recurring products or services that require customer effort to produce results, and whose ordinary free trials draw tire-kickers who never become paying customers.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Calling it a "penalty." Just call it a free trial.
- Charging customers who never began. Collecting a minor fee does not justify a one-star review.
- Making money from fees. The profit should come from results that turn into paying customers.
- Tuning: if nobody takes the trial, lower the requirements or fees. If they take it but don't follow through, explain the fees better.

## The math
Closing 3 of 10 on the main offer, downselling 4 more into the trial, then converting 3 of those doubles sales from 3 to 6 (p. 122). Split fees per missed item ($50 each on a $500 product) rather than one lump sum, unless a single miss wrecks their results (p. 124). A $1 first month justifies taking a card and tests that it works (p. 129).
