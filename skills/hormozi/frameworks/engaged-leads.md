# Engaged Leads
slug: engaged-leads · category: leads · aliases: what is a lead, lead vs engaged lead, raised hands

## What it is
Hormozi's working definitions for the whole book. A lead is simply anyone you are able to contact: a bought email list, numbers in your phone, people walking past on the street. That on its own is not the goal. The real output of advertising is the engaged lead, someone who has shown interest in what you sell, for example by leaving contact details on a page, following you where you can reach them, or replying to your message.

## How to apply it
- Stop counting clicks, impressions, or raw list size as "leads" in your reporting. Count people who raised their hand.
- Design every campaign around a clear engagement action: opt in, reply, book, claim.
- Get engagement with either your core offer or a lead magnet (see [[lead-magnets]]).
- Be clear on where this stops: the book's job ends at a raised hand. Turning that interest into a purchase is sales, a separate problem.

## When to reach for it
- A team argues about lead numbers because everyone means something different.
- Marketing reports lots of "leads" but sales says they are junk.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Loose vocabulary. He argues fuzzy words produce fuzzy thinking, which produces wrong actions.
- Confusing "getting strangers to show interest" with "getting strangers to buy". He deliberately tested and rejected a book subtitle that promised buying, because that is a sales promise, not a leads promise (p. 63).

## The math
No formula here; the point is choosing the right numerator. Engaged leads, not contacts, are what every later cost and conversion metric in the book is measured against.
