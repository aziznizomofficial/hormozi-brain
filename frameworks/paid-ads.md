# Run Paid Ads (the four-step ad system)
slug: paid-ads · category: leads · aliases: run paid ads, paid advertising, needles in the haystack, puddle-pond-lake-ocean, one-to-many cold

## What it is
Paid ads are the fourth of Hormozi's "core four" lead methods: renting other people's audiences to put your offer in front of strangers, one-to-many. Reach is bought and guaranteed; getting the money back is not. So the whole game is efficiency. He frames it as a haystack search: start with everyone, then keep narrowing until a large share of who is left are your ideal buyers. Four problems to solve: where to advertise, getting the right audience to see it, making the ad, and getting permission to contact them.

## How to apply it
1. **Pick one platform** that passes four tests: you have used it and got value as a consumer, it lets you target your buyers, you know its ad formats, and you can afford its minimum spend. Start consuming its ads on purpose.
2. **Target.** Build a lookalike from your best list (customers first; top up with warm, then cold outreach lists to hit the platform minimum), then layer filters (age, location, interests, timing) on top. No lookalike possible? Start with interests.
3. **Make the ad** with three parts: callout, value, call to action (see [[ad-callouts]], [[what-who-when-ad-framework]]).
4. **Get contact info** on a simple landing page that looks and speaks like the ad that sent them, and use consistency ("you did A, so do B") to push them to the next step. You are not selling here, just asking whether they want to hear more.

## When to reach for it
When warm/cold outreach and content already work and you have some cash to trade for speed and scale.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Blaming the ad when the real problem is the wrong people saw it.
- Landing pages that don't match the ad (a "Frankenstein" experience).
- Long, forgettable URLs and fuzzy CTAs.
- Staying stuck: he lost four years afraid to build a landing page, then finished one before lunch.

## The math
Win small and specific, then widen: puddle, pond, lake, ocean. Ratios fall as audiences broaden, but total profit rises. His example: $1k in, $10k back ($9k profit) versus $100k in, $300k back ($200k profit). Filters raise efficiency but burn through an audience faster.

Origin (pp. 187–189): his first ad was plain all-caps text offering five locals a free six-week challenge for before/after photos. $1,000 spent, 19 sales at $299 (about $5,700). The offer, not polish, did the work.
