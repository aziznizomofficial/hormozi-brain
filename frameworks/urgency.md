# Urgency
slug: urgency · category: offer · aliases: deadlines drive decisions, rolling cohorts, seasonal promotions, exploding offer, price-change urgency

## What it is
Scarcity limits how many can buy; urgency limits when. A real deadline lowers the threshold for action, pushing people to do what they already intended. He uses it constantly and insists it be honest: you control the calendar, so there is always a legitimate deadline available.

## How to apply it
His four ethical forms:
1. Cohort-based rolling urgency: start clients in groups on a fixed cadence (weekly, monthly, quarterly). Buy now and you start Monday; otherwise wait for the next kickoff. The rarer the start dates, the stronger the pull. It also forces a choreographed onboarding.
2. Rolling seasonal urgency: run the same core promotion under a new seasonal name each month with a visible end date on every page and ad. His top play for local businesses.
3. Pricing or bonus urgency: make the promotion, discount or bonus expire, not the service itself (a roofer will still serve you later; the deal will not).
4. Exploding opportunity: when the opportunity itself decays (an arbitrage, an early ad platform), stress that each day of delay costs them.

If someone buys right after a cohort starts: either give a fast personal onboarding as a bonus, or frame the wait as extra prep time plus a longer payment plan.

## When to reach for it
- Leads say "let me think about it" and never return.
- Year-round sales with no natural deadline.
- Before any genuine price increase: announce it to flush fence-sitters from the pipeline.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Fake countdowns; they destroy credibility.
- Fearing you lose sales by turning people away; people who would not act under a deadline were not going to buy anyway.
- Raising prices without telling anyone first.

## The math
In a week-long launch, the final four hours often bring 50-60% of sales: about 3% of the window producing over half the revenue.
