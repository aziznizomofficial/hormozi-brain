# Agencies: Hire to Learn, Then Replace
slug: agencies · category: scaling · aliases: learn-from-the-agency model, agency apprenticeship, good-enough then elite agency, agency checklist

## What it is
The third lead getter: service businesses you pay to run ads, outreach or content. Used the way agencies want (outsource forever), results decay: the senior rep moves to the newest client, a junior takes over, you cancel and repeat elsewhere. The way that worked for him was his very first one: in 2016 he paid a Facebook ads expert $750/hour to coach him weekly while he drove, and after about eight sessions (roughly $6,000) he could run the ads himself, a skill he says made him millions.

## How to apply it
- **Use agencies for two things:** learning a new method and learning a new platform. With no money, learn by trial and error instead.
- **Open with a purpose and a deadline.** In your own words, tell them: I want to do this in-house; I'll work with you about six months, pay extra for you to explain your decisions and steps, train my team on it, then move to a cheaper consulting arrangement. Negotiate; at some price it works for both sides.
- **Run both teams in parallel** and compare. Your team is full time on you while the agency's attention thins with each new client. When yours beats theirs regularly, drop to discounted consulting, then cut ties when you're teaching them.
- **Two-agency pattern:** a "good enough" agency to learn the basics of a new platform, then a pricier elite one to learn how to max it out. For YouTube he hired one to keep him shipping and a second at 4x the price to teach craft, then dropped to consulting once his videos beat theirs.

**Picking one (his 10 signs):** results for someone you know; recognisable clients; a waitlist; realistic expectations in sales; long-term strategy and clear timelines, no hacks; clear asks of you; regular meetings and updates; simple cost-vs-results tracking; a strong offer by the value equation; expensive (but expensive doesn't guarantee good). Interview several even after one agrees.

## When to reach for it
You have cash, want a new channel or platform, and don't want years of trial and error.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Believing you'll never need to learn it because they do it.
- Hiring agencies found only through their own ads or cold outreach; the best tend to live on word of mouth.
- Being cheap; budget for a stretch of paying both the agency and your team.

## The math
His time to get an in-house team matching an agency fell from about a year to ten months, eight, and now under six.
