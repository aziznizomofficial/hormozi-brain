# Best Customer Avatar (Top-20% Survey)
slug: best-customer-avatar · category: marketing · aliases: your first avatar, find your best customers, Vista method, customer segmentation, re-engineer the buying process, quality over quantity

## What it is
Borrowed from a private-equity pricing specialist: score existing customers by how much they pay and how long they stay, cut the channels that bring low-value customers, and pour effort into the ones that bring high-value customers. Because a fifth of customers tend to produce most of the revenue, replacing the rest with more of the best can multiply the business. You also copy how the best customers came to buy and make every prospect go through it.

## How to apply it
1. Survey customers (live at an event or on a call gets better response; tie completion to a small benefit). Ask about demographics, their numbers before and now, what they hoped to achieve, and how they bought: main reason, trigger, content consumed, time from first contact to purchase, referral source.
2. Sort for the best: the ones you like most, who spent most and stayed longest. Keep the top 20%, ignore the rest.
3. Find the fewest shared traits, usually three to five qualifiers.
4. Execute: state requirements openly in ads and pages, speak only to those people's problems and aspirations, stop selling anyone who does not qualify, and push harder on the channels they come from.
5. Re-engineer the buying process: recreate the steps your best customers took before buying and build them into every lead's journey.

## When to reach for it
- High churn, low satisfaction and generic advice because you accept anyone with a card.
- Rising acquisition costs with thin lifetime value.
- Marketing and sales blaming each other over lead quality.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Panicking and stripping qualification steps for volume; he found that raised lead count but lowered money made.
- Judging by short-term revenue; narrowing may dip revenue before retention and margin climb.

## The math
Gym Launch found 78% of top customers had consumed at least two long-form content pieces before buying, so sales began sending two pieces from a "greatest hits" list to every lead. A competitor in the same vertical with the same sales volume made about 70x less profit; competitor LTVs ran about $6,000-8,000 versus Gym Launch's $45,000+. He would pay $5,000 to acquire $45,000 before $1,000 to acquire $5,000.
