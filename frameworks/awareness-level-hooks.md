# Awareness-Level Hooks (Expansion Hooks)
slug: awareness-level-hooks · category: marketing · aliases: levels of awareness, Schwartz pyramid, expansion hooks, offer/proof/promise/pain/curiosity hooks

## What it is
Hormozi borrows Eugene Schwartz's five stages of customer awareness to explain why ads hit a ceiling and how to push past it. Ads start by converting the smallest, warmest group, the people who already know you. Scaling means reaching larger and colder groups, which need a different kind of hook. He treats the stages as a continuum rather than neat boxes: the stronger the creative (hook, offer, messenger), the further up into cold traffic it can convert.

## How to apply it
Match the hook's driver to how aware the audience is:
1. **Most aware** (knows your product, just needs the deal): **offer-driven** hooks. Discounts, new versions, a clear incentive to act.
2. **Product aware** (knows the kind of product, not that you are the one to buy from): **proof-driven** hooks. Numbers of customers and specific results.
3. **Solution aware** (knows the result they want, not that you deliver it): **promise-driven** hooks. A big, specific outcome with a timeframe.
4. **Problem aware** (feels the pain, does not know a solution exists): **pain-driven** hooks. Name the frustration and hint at a better way.
5. **Completely unaware:** **curiosity-driven** hooks. Point to a hidden cost or danger they did not know about.

Then:
- When writing your ~50 hooks, spread them across these levels. If nearly all of them are offer hooks, you are only reaching the warm slice.
- When unsure, go a little broader. You still catch the warm audience and pick up some cold.
- For the widest reach, test memes, or memes specific to your niche's culture.

## When to reach for it
- You have refreshed your creative and still cannot spend more profitably.
- You are entering a new market or a colder audience.
- Your hooks are all offer- and proof-heavy.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Copying a big brand's offer-only ad. It works for them because everyone already knows the brand and the product. For an unknown company whose product needs explaining, it burns money.
- Treating the levels as rigid rules. They are a toolkit, and reality is messier than the labels.
