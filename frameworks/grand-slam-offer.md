# Grand Slam Offer
slug: grand-slam-offer · category: offer · aliases: irresistible offer, offer so good people feel stupid saying no, category of one, selling in a vacuum, the commodity problem

## What it is
An offer is everything you exchange for money: what you deliver, how you get paid, and the terms. A Grand Slam Offer is one that cannot be compared with anything else on the market, because it bundles an attractive promotion, a value proposition nobody matches, a premium price, a strong guarantee and payment terms that fund acquisition. Once prospects cannot compare you, they buy on value instead of price. The fulfillment can be the same work a commodity competitor does; only the packaging changes.

## How to apply it
1. Diagnose commoditization: if a prospect can hold your offer next to a rival's and pick the cheaper one, you are a commodity priced at "market efficiency" (just enough margin to survive).
2. Pick a market that can pay ([[starving-crowd]], [[niche-down]]).
3. Price at a premium ([[virtuous-cycle-of-price]]).
4. Build value with the [[value-equation]] and the [[offer-creation-process]].
5. Enhance it with [[scarcity]], [[urgency]], [[bonuses]], [[guarantees]] and a strong name ([[magic-naming-formula]]).

## When to reach for it
- Prospects shop you on price or ask "why are you more expensive?"
- Too few clients and too little cash at the same time; margins thin after discounting.
- Your ads look and sound like every competitor's.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Explaining why you are different instead of letting the offer make the difference obvious.
- Copying the market's standard structure: pay for effort, with results left to chance.
- Handing a great offer to the wrong audience; it falls flat.
- Treating a failed offer as personal failure. Try again; it takes many attempts.

## The math
Same ad spend, three multipliers: more people respond, more of them buy, and each pays more. In his lead-gen agency example the pay-for-performance offer got roughly 2.5x the response, 2.5x the conversion and a 4x upfront price; he tallies it at about 22x more cash collected up front ($10,000 in ads returning about $112,000), versus the retainer offer that lost half its ad spend in month one and broke even in month two.
