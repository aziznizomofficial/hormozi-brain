# Six-Point Testimonial Script (and Event Capture)
slug: six-point-testimonial-script · category: marketing · aliases: testimonial template, internal/external struggle-victory, pro testimonials, man-on-the-street, stage ads, event ads

## What it is
The interview structure Hormozi's teams use for every recorded testimonial in the Marketing Machine: at events, in bonus unlocks, award claims and competitions. It follows the customer from inner and outer struggle, through doubt, to outer and inner victory, so the story makes sense and addresses the viewer's own hesitation. Events are where he captures the most of these. Even when nothing is sold at an event, the footage alone can pay for it.

## How to apply it
**The six questions:**
1. **Internal struggle:** what was rock bottom? What did you hate doing, or wish you could do?
2. **External struggle:** what were your hard numbers then?
3. **Skepticism:** what worried you about buying or joining?
4. **Overcome:** what made you go ahead anyway?
5. **External victory:** what are the numbers now?
6. **Internal victory:** best moment since? What can you do now, or no longer have to do?
Get an explicit hook first (try a few), and have them say their name on camera so the footage can be matched later.

**Three event formats:**
- **Stage ads:** photo or video from the stage with the crowd celebrating behind you, ideally at the end of day one or before lunch on day two. You get one take.
- **Pro testimonials:** a separate recording room, your better camera team, tested lights and audio, and a pre-selected, confirmed hit list on a clipboard. Announce the process at the start and introduce the handler, avoid keynotes (catch people at breaks), give customers a bonus for taking part, and pay the handler extra for anyone recorded who was not on the list.
- **Man-on-the-street:** a second crew asks one question at a time (for example, how many new customers since you started?) until they have 10+ answers per question. Or they run all six questions rapid-fire in under two minutes. Cut these into fast highlight reels.

## When to reach for it
- Testimonials ramble or skip the numbers.
- You run in-person events, or could run 1–2 a year.
- Proof covers the win but not the doubt the prospect feels.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Not testing audio and video, then losing a whole event's recordings.
- Skipping the hook. It is the most important part of the testimonial.
- Pulling people out during keynotes.

## The math
Man-on-the-street target: 10 core questions × 10+ answers each.
