# Lead Magnets
slug: lead-magnets · category: leads · aliases: seven steps to a lead magnet, salty pretzel, problem-solution cycle, give away the secrets sell the implementation

## What it is
Advertise your core offer first; it is the direct route. When buyers want to know more before paying (common with expensive offers), a lead magnet usually engages more people. It is a complete, cheap or free solution to one narrow problem that, once solved, exposes the next problem, which your core offer fixes. His analogy: free salty bar snacks that make people thirsty for a paid drink.

## How to apply it
The seven steps:
1. Pick a narrow, meaningful problem (problem-solution cycle: each solution reveals a new problem; choose one whose next problem your core offer solves).
2. Pick the type: reveal their problem (diagnosis), sample or trial (recurring problems), or one step of a multi-step process (complex problems).
3. Pick delivery: software, information, free service, or physical product. Up to twelve combinations; build several and rotate.
4. Test the name: headline, then image, then subheadline, via polls or "reply 1 or 2" posts.
5. Make it easy to consume: every format, device and time slot.
6. Make it truly good: give away the secrets, sell the implementation.
7. Make it easy to ask for more with a clear call to action.

## When to reach for it
- High-ticket or considered purchases where cold prospects will not buy on first contact.
- Your ads for the core offer get attention but little engagement.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Handing out fluff to protect "secrets"; prospects judge you by your free stuff and tell others.
- Overbuilding: his webinar flopped; a 13-minute recorded case study filled the calendar (pp. 43–47).

## The math
Worked example (pp. 69–70): $10,000 profit per sale; direct ads cost $1,000 per call with a 1-in-3 close, so $3,000 per customer. A free magnet costing $25 to deliver drops a call to about $100 all in; at 1-in-10 buying, acquisition falls to $1,000, a 10:1 return on the same budget. A good magnet engages ideal buyers, beats the core offer on engagement, gets consumed, and makes the right people likelier to buy (p. 71).

Packaging and presentation changes can multiply engagement 2–10x (p. 59); offering more formats can lift take rates 2–4x (p. 64). Aim for the free thing to deliver more value than the price of the core offer. "Pays with their time now" (p. 49) is his shorthand: whoever invests time today is likelier to invest money later.
