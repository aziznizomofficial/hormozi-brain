# Churn & the Leaky Bucket
slug: churn · category: retention · aliases: churn rate, retention math, leaky bucket business, revolving door, structural churn, shaking the tree

## What it is
Churn is the share of customers at the start of a period who are gone by the end of it. Hormozi sees high churn as a hidden growth ceiling. A "leaky bucket" business has to keep acquiring just to stay the same size, never compounds, is hard to sell, and builds bad word of mouth. Existing customers already trust you, have already paid, cost nothing to acquire and fit your ideal profile, so keeping them is the cheapest growth available.

## How to apply it
1. Measure monthly churn correctly: only the starting group counts, and new sign-ups don't change it.
2. Turn it into lifespan and LTV: average months ≈ 1 ÷ churn, and LTGP ≈ monthly gross profit ÷ churn.
3. Set a retention budget. Spending up to a fifth of CAC to keep a customer goes a long way.
4. Ask the reverse question: what would make every customer leave? (Ignoring them, broken promises, poor communication, unrealistic expectations, hiding progress, isolating them, making the product hard to use.) Then do the opposite.
5. Aim for referrals to exceed churn. When they do, the business grows even without other acquisition.
6. Work through the [[churn-checklist]] and [[five-horsemen-of-retention]].

## When to reach for it
You market constantly but stay flat, a subscription loses members as fast as it gains them, or you're preparing a business for sale.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Assuming you can always outsell churn. His gym clients at 15% monthly churn lost 83% of members a year and failed if they fell even two sales behind each month.
- Believing more deliverables reduce churn. Overwhelm is the main cause, so aim for less but better.
- Excusing small-business churn. Serving very small businesses brings built-in churn as they fail, but Shopify and Skool show you can still reduce it and build large companies.

## The math
- Churn from 10% to 3% means a customer stays about 33 months instead of 10: 3.3x LTV (Retention p. 13).
- Keeping a customer costs 5–25x less than acquiring one. A Harvard Business Review finding: 5% better retention raised profit 25–95% (p. 14).
- Referrals of 10% against churn of 10% means flat growth. Improve either one and you grow (p. 14).
- He has yet to see a business under 3% monthly churn that isn't very profitable (p. 8).
