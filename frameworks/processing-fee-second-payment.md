# Processing Fee and Second Form of Payment
slug: processing-fee-second-payment · category: retention · aliases: credit card processing fee, backup card, involuntary churn, multi-form payment

## What it is
A two-part checkout script. First, add a card processing fee (around 3.99%) after the customer agrees to the price. If they push back, waive the fee in exchange for a second, backup payment method. Either outcome wins: the fee adds revenue, and the backup card cuts involuntary churn from declined or changed cards, which is worth far more to lifetime value than the fee.

## How to apply it
1. After agreement, ask how they want to pay.
2. Mention the card processing fee plainly.
3. If they hesitate, offer to drop it if they add a second payment method, explaining that declined cards (about 7% a month, he says) cost you time to chase, and you pass the savings on.
4. Have them authorize both methods.
5. Retry any failed charge the next day on the backup method.
6. For public menus, note the processing fee at the bottom.

## When to reach for it
- Recurring billing where failed payments quietly drain customers.
- Thin margins where a few percent of revenue matters.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Thinking waiving the fee erases the gain; the backup card is the bigger prize.
- Fearing lost sales; he says he has never seen a sale lost to adding the fee.

## The math
Involuntary churn from card changes runs about 1.2-1.7% per month; at 5% total monthly churn that is 24-34% of all churn. On a $100/mo service with 5% churn (LTV $2,000): accepting the fee lifts LTV to $2,080 (+4%); a backup card that cuts churn to 3.8% lifts LTV to about $2,631 (+31%), or to $3,030 (+51%) at 3.3%. At 10% churn the gains are smaller but real (about +14% to +20%). The friend who taught him this said a 3.99% fee added about a third to his agency's profit.
