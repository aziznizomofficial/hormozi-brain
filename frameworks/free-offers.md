# Free Offers (and the friction dial)
slug: free-offers · category: offer · aliases: free promotions, free front end, penny gap, friction levers, freebie seekers myth

## What it is
Hormozi calls free the most powerful offer there is: value in advance, something for nothing. He cites Dan Ariely's "penny gap" (about 9x more people took a free chocolate than one priced at a penny). Free is also the fastest test of whether anyone wants your thing. If a free offer flops, it means one of three things: they don't want it (change it or how you describe it), they don't believe it (explain *why* it's free), or the wrong people are seeing it (targeting).

## How to apply it
**Pros:** most leads per eyeball (key in small local markets); lowest cost per lead; giants from Facebook and Dropbox to Uber, Netflix and Public Storage grew on free front ends.
**Cons and fixes:** volume can swamp you, and some people only want the freebie. Answer with friction, tuned to a sweet spot that screens out time-wasters without losing lazy big buyers:
1. **Qualifications** stated everywhere (copy, creative, landing page).
2. **More or heavier questions**: phone or income asks weigh more than a first name; open-ended weighs more than multiple choice.
3. **More steps**: each one sheds people.
4. **Forced consumption**: his favourite, e.g. a long video before any CTA appears, or content between first and second meetings. Best when eyeballs are cheap.
5. **Longer ads**: longer copy or video before the CTA raises quality.
Design the freebie so it gives real value without overextending you.

## When to reach for it
You need volume fast, are new to a market, or have an empty calendar. He says if he could make only one offer, it would be free: better to sort bad leads than stare at an empty calendar.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Believing free attracts broke people. Across four split tests of free vs paid front ends, each in ten markets, gym close rates and ticket sizes were the same; going free usually cut lead cost by five times or more.
- Judging campaigns by the share of qualified leads instead of the absolute count.
- Offers so generous they're unbelievable; always answer "why is this free?"

## The math
$1,000 of ads: campaign A gets 500 leads, 250 qualified; campaign B gets 200 leads, 160 qualified. B feels better but A wins on qualified leads per dollar.
