# Menu Upsell
slug: menu-upsell · category: money-model · aliases: menu close, unselling, prescription upsell, A/B upsell, card on file, You Don't Need That…You Need This

## What it is
A consultative upsell that runs in four moves. First, tell customers what they **don't** need. Then prescribe what they **do** need and exactly how to use it. Then ask which of two options they **prefer**. Finally, suggest the **card on file**. Taking items off the list builds trust, and at no point does the customer face a plain buy/don't-buy question. Hormozi found these tactics while selling supplements at his gym, then used them for thousands of customers.

## How to apply it
1. **Unsell:** cross off options that don't fit this customer. That can be lower-margin items, which pushes attention to higher-margin ones.
2. **Prescribe:** say how the product fits with what they already bought, and give personal, specific instructions (dose, timing, sequence) as if they already own it. Writing it on the order form works.
3. **A/B:** ask about preference, not about buying: flavor, quantity, start date, time slot, person, delivery speed, and so on. Almost anything can be framed as A or B. For newcomers, add a nudge such as "this is my favorite" (p. 92).
4. **Card on file:** ask whether to use the card you already have. For a first purchase, ask which card they'd like to use. This removes the small frictions of paying.
5. Out of stock? Take payment now and set a delivery date.

## When to reach for it
You have several products or tiers, customers feel overwhelmed or suspect a sales pitch, and staff (who enjoy helping customers game the system) can be trained on a script.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Pitching features and science instead of asking what they prefer (his 19-consult, zero-sales day).
- Vague, generic suggestions. Detailed, personal instructions sell far more.
- Giving them a chance to say "no" when they could be choosing between two ways to say "yes."

## The math
The Economist play: offering digital for $59 and digital+print for $125 made print collapse. Adding print-only at $125, the same price as the bundle, pushed buyers to the bundle. When you have options A and B, add "both" at the price of the more expensive one (p. 93).
