# Open to Goal (+ one-page advertising checklist)
slug: open-to-goal · category: mindset · aliases: rule of 100 on steroids, work until the outcome, one page advertising plan, habit stack

## What it is
The upgrade to the [[rule-of-100]]. Instead of committing to a number of actions, you commit to a number of **outcomes** and keep working until you hit them, however long it takes. Name comes from a gym chain that let sales managers set their own hours, provided each signed five new members a day: done by lunch, go home; needs 18 hours, stay 18. Hormozi says elite salespeople and founders across industries run some version of it. Give up "doing your best"; do what is required.

## How to apply it
**Protect the block.** His highest-return habits: wake at 4–5 am (which really means going to bed early), start work immediately with no rituals, and take no meetings before noon. The value is a long uninterrupted work stretch right after a long uninterrupted sleep. He sets the daily goal from what he knows he can do, and only handles fires and people after the block. Too much at first? Scale it back a few hours and build up.

**One-page advertising checklist (about five minutes to fill):**
1. Pick the engaged-lead type to go after: customers, affiliates, employees, or agencies.
2. Pick rule of 100 or open to goal, and commit to daily advertising actions.
3. Fill in the advertising checklist for that daily action.
4. Repeat daily until you can afford to pay someone else to do it.
5. Then go back to step 1 with employees as the lead type; repeat until you have enough help, then scale again.

## When to reach for it
Once 100 actions a day has become normal and you want the next level; any time output is falling short of a fixed target.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Underestimating required volume. His first flyer test was 300 flyers; a mentor tested with 5,000 and ran winners at 5,000 a day for a month (half a percent response is decent, one percent a winner). Hormozi reckons he was doing 1/1500th of what was needed.
- "100 reach-outs over six weeks" is not the rule; the rule is 100 per day.
- Hundred-page plans nobody uses. One page leaves no room for excuses: you did it or you didn't.

## The math
300 flyers at 0.5% = 1.5 responses, too few to judge anything. Quote: "Do more than they do, and you will have more than they have." (p. 365)
