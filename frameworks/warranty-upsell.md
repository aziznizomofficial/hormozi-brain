# Priced Guarantee / Warranty Upsell
slug: warranty-upsell · category: money-model · aliases: guarantee and warranty upsells, sell the guarantee, priced guarantee, AppleCare play, buying and selling risk

## What it is
Instead of giving a guarantee away, sell it as a small add-on right after the customer commits. People hate risk and will pay you to carry it, so a warranty or outcome guarantee priced at a slice of the purchase becomes nearly pure profit. He took the idea from Apple, which once replaced damaged phones free and later built a multi-billion-dollar business charging for that coverage. In one of his portfolio companies selling high-end physical products, the ten-year warranty that had been standard was sold for 10% of the price, and many buyers took it.

## How to apply it
1. After the buyer agrees, ask casually whether they want the standard warranty. When they ask, explain it covers them if anything goes wrong and that many people choose it.
2. Price it at 5-30% of the product; with high margins, price it at or above your cost to redo or replace, so a claim never loses money.
3. When someone makes a claim, check whether they bought the coverage.
4. You can still make goodwill exceptions for non-buyers, as you probably do already for free.
5. Use the added revenue to offset sales commissions.

## When to reach for it
- Main-street and traditional businesses selling products or services with occasional failures.
- You already honor refunds or replacements without charging for that security.
- Low-priced items too: shipping insurance shows people pay for risk removal at any price.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Mispricing the risk: you are an underwriter now, so know your claim rate and the cost of each claim.
- Assuming it only works on expensive items.

## The math
A $1,000 item with a $100 warranty costing $100 to honor, claimed by 1 in 20 buyers: 20 x $100 minus $100 = $1,900 extra profit per 20 warranties sold. His table: a $1,000 table with a $100 no-watermark warranty, 45% take rate and 5% claims adds about $43 per customer (+4.3% revenue). If half of customers take a 10% add-on, you recover 5% of revenue, about half of a typical 10% sales commission.
