# Three Phases of Scaling Paid Ads
slug: paid-ads-scaling-phases · category: scaling · aliases: track money / lose money / print money, ad testing budget, reverse the ad budget

## What it is
Hormozi's answer to "how much should I spend on ads?" Paid ads are a return-on-investment game, not a creativity contest: get them good enough to scale and stop polishing. Spending moves through three phases: track money, lose money (really: invest in building a money machine), print money.

## How to apply it
1. **Track.** Before the first dollar, set up tracking so you can see returns per ad. Without it you are gambling for as long as you feel like it rather than as long as you can afford. Hire help or learn it; just get it done.
2. **Lose (invest).** Expect most tests to fail. Cut losers quickly, pour money onto the rare winner. His test budget rule: let a new ad spend up to **2x the cash a new customer brings in within 30 days** (not lifetime) as long as it produces leads; kill it at **1x** that figure if it produces no leads at all.
3. **Print.** Once ads break even or better, flip the question from "how much should I spend?" to "how many customers do I want or can I handle?" Work the budget backwards from that, pad it for declining efficiency, divide into a daily spend, and commit.

Also from his paid-ads lessons: place a $100 ad on any platform just to get in the game; pick a monthly amount you accept losing while learning; do paid ads last among the core four, because the skills from the other three carry over and they fund it.

## When to reach for it
Setting or defending an ad budget; deciding when to kill or scale an ad.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Letting losing ads run too long, and (costlier) quitting on ads before they had a fair chance.
- Judging returns week to week. One company he worked with took a year to make ads profitable, then recovered the whole year's spend in the following month.
- Saying "I'm not techy." He said it for four years; four focused hours fixed it.
- Treating a sales problem as an ad problem (see [[ltgp-to-cac]]).

## The math
- Ten $100 tests: nine lose everything, one returns $500. Still down $500, but scale that winner 100x ($10k in, $50k out).
- Budget reverse: want 100 customers at $100 CAC = $10,000, plus ~20% padding = $12,000/month = $400/day.
- Test cap: $100 thirty-day profit per customer means a $200 test limit (or $100 with zero leads).
