# Calls To Action
slug: calls-to-action · category: marketing · aliases: CTA, reason why, scarcity and urgency, fraternity party planner

## What it is
A call to action tells the audience exactly what to do next to become an engaged lead. Hormozi says a good one has two parts: the action itself, and reasons to take it now. Reasons increase response even when weak, and good reasons beat weak ones. His three favourite reasons are scarcity, urgency, and a made-up occasion he nicknames the "fraternity party planner".

## How to apply it
- The action: plain and direct wording ("call now", "book the call", "click the button"), not vague phrasing. Clarity over cleverness.
- Scarcity: limited supply. His preferred version is the honest one. Find your real capacity limit (onboarding slots, inventory, hours per week) and advertise it.
- Urgency: limited time. Close a promotion, bonus, or discount at a set deadline, even if supply is unlimited.
- Invented reason: attach any occasion to the ask (a holiday, a birthday, a themed day). He cites a queue-cutting study where giving any reason raised compliance, with sensible reasons doing best.
- Stack as many truthful reasons as you can, and give the CTA often, not just once at the end.
- In ads (pp. 211–212): interest peaks briefly, so spell the step out literally (press this, call this, reply with a word, scan this) and make it frictionless: obvious buttons, easy numbers, short memorable web addresses. He paid $370,000 for a one-word domain and thinks most people undervalue easy URLs. Urgency, scarcity and bonuses apply to ad CTAs too.
- GOATed Ads version (pp. 23–24): a complete ad CTA covers what to do, how, when, what they get, and (for lead magnets and multi-step funnels) what happens next. Then *show* it: walk through the click, form or booking on screen so what they see after clicking matches what they were promised.
- Record only 1–3 CTAs per ad session. To test, run the same hook and body to the same audience and change only the CTA wording. Keep the first clear one that works.

## When to reach for it
- The end of every lead magnet, piece of content, ad, or outreach message.
- Engagement is decent but few people take the next step.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Leaving the next step implied instead of stated.
- Faking scarcity. Real constraints make ethical scarcity; use the limits you already have.
- Making supply so scarce you cap how many leads you can collect.
- Over-engineering it. A decent CTA has never sunk a campaign, but a missing one has (GOATed Ads p. 24).

## The math
Shorter windows produce faster, bunched action; fewer units raise perceived value but also cap volume. No fixed conversion figures are given in this section. In GOATed Ads, CTAs get roughly 0% of ad prep time, against 80% for hooks and 20% for the body (p. 9).
