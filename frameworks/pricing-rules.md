# Hormozi's Rules of Pricing
slug: pricing-rules · category: pricing · aliases: when to raise prices, high close rate means prices too low, hear more nos, customer surplus, one-time vs ongoing value pricing, tiered pricing by avatar

## What it is
The beliefs under his pricing tactics. Core idea: raising prices both raises gross profit per customer and lowers delivery costs (fewer customers to serve), so price usually moves profit more than anything else. The emotional cost is hearing "no" more often, which is why most owners cave.

## How to apply it
Signals that you are underpriced:
- You close more than about half of sales consistently.
- You are at full capacity, or have fixed capacity (e.g., a painter who can only make ten pieces a month) and sell out.

Operating rules:
1. Price for lifetime value, not the first sale; he now wants the price people keep paying, not the highest they will pay once.
2. Keep raising until the extra money from each sale no longer covers the lost sales.
3. Raise value alongside price; bigger jumps need bigger improvements.
4. You have gone too far when sales stop or customers complain about value (measure with NPS).
5. Different customer types get different prices; one avatar may pay 5-10x another. Offer tiers, make it obvious which tier is for whom, and name tiers after an identity the customer aspires to.
6. Price one-time value (information) separately from ongoing value (accountability); bundling them under-prices one and over-prices the other.
7. Leave customer surplus: charge a premium, then use the margin to deliver so much that customers still feel they won.

## When to reach for it
- High close rates, waitlists, or a calendar that is always full.
- Thin margins despite steady demand.
- One price for very different customers.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Going back to the cheaper price the moment rejections rise.
- Mixing one-time and recurring value in a single recurring fee, which invites cancellation once the information is learned.

## The math
His gym: tripled price from $99 to $299 and lost 30% of members; revenue went from 200 x $99 (about $19,800) to 140 x $299 (about $41,900) while delivery costs fell 30%. Going from a 50% to 30% close rate while doubling price still earns more.
