# Culture of Execution (Sales Team)
slug: culture-of-execution · category: sales · aliases: do the boring work, volume negates luck, yellows are the new gold, garbage man, process over all, rank reps by show/close

## What it is
Hormozi's point that knowing the lead-nurture tactics is worth nothing unless the team actually works leads every day, and that culture is what makes them do it. He defines culture simply as the rules an organisation uses to reward and punish behaviour. The goal is to reward being available, fast, personal and persistent, and to reject being unavailable, slow, generic and fragile. The story behind it is Jacob, a teenager Hormozi put on a cold-call team. Jacob aimed to double the top rep's call volume, took everyone's less-qualified "yellow" leads on evenings and weekends, and finished as the top salesman before becoming Director of Sales at Acquisition.com.

## How to apply it
1. **Pay for it:** add a small commission bump for show rates, around 5–30% of what a rep earns per sale depending on ticket size.
2. **Reward it socially** with attention, affection and approval. Praise the reps with the best show rates, ask them how they did it, and praise them again when the answer is "I followed the process." Call out high closers who will not work their leads as entitled.
3. **Rank every rep on three lists:** show rate (work ethic), close rate (skill), and overall lead-to-close conversion. A healthy team has the same names at the top of all three.
4. **Spread the sayings** that reinforce the behaviour: do the boring work, volume negates luck, speed is king, BAMFAM as a way of life, yellows are gold, be a "garbage man" who works leads others dismiss, every lead is free practice, and no one is above the process.
5. **Hire and develop through setting.** He found the best closers are usually the best setters, so setting is a low-risk way to start new reps.

## When to reach for it
- Reps cherry-pick leads and let the rest rot.
- Management tracks only close rate.
- The lead-nurture playbook is known but not followed.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Being obsessed with close rate alone. What matters is conversion of all the leads you have.
- Judging leads by how they look. Some who look broke are wealthy and appreciate not being judged.
- Treating unqualified leads as wasted time instead of practice.

## The math
Jacob's rule of thumb for catching the leader: if the top rep makes 200 calls a day, make 400. If they work five days, work six. Matching them only keeps you behind.
