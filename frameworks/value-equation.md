# Value Equation
slug: value-equation · category: offer · aliases: four value drivers, dream outcome, perceived likelihood of achievement, time delay, effort and sacrifice, fast beats free

## What it is
His formula for why anything is worth paying for. Two drivers raise value (the dream outcome and the buyer's belief they will actually get it) and two drag it down (how long they wait and how much effort or sacrifice it costs). It is written as a fraction on purpose: push the bottom toward zero and value heads toward infinity. What counts is the buyer's perception of each driver, not the objective reality.

## How to apply it
1. Dream outcome: reflect the prospect's desired end state back to them, framed in status (how others will see them).
2. Perceived likelihood: raise certainty with proof, track record, what you include or leave out, and guarantees. People pay for certainty (the 10,000th patient beats the first).
3. Time delay: shorten the wait to the result and engineer fast early wins (e.g., first sale inside a week) so buyers stay long enough to reach the big outcome.
4. Effort and sacrifice: remove work; done-for-you beats do-it-yourself on price.
5. Use the four drivers as a checklist for listing problems, trimming deliverables, and naming bonuses.

## When to reach for it
- Customers say "not worth it", "won't work for me", "too hard", "no time".
- Two offers promise the same result and yours earns less: the dream outcome cancels out, so the other three drivers decide.
- You compete against free: speed wins.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Only inflating the top of the equation with bigger claims; that is easy and everyone does it. The best companies win on the bottom half.
- Assuming improvements matter if the buyer cannot perceive them (the London Tube map cut perceived waiting more than faster trains).
- Arguing with human nature about what people ought to value.

## The math
Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice). If the denominator approaches zero, value becomes effectively unlimited. Market evidence he cites: supplements (~$123B) are twice the health-club industry (~$62B), and liposuction commands about $25,000 while a bootcamp struggles at $100/mo, because of the bottom half.
