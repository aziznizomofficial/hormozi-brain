# Hooks (Call-Out + Condition for Value)
slug: hooks · category: marketing · aliases: hook anatomy, what's a hook, call out, cocktail party effect, verbal and nonverbal hooks, hook types

## What it is
A hook is whatever a person sees or hears first in an ad, post, email or outreach message: the opening seconds, line or image. Its only job is to sell the viewer on paying attention, so they recognise it is meant for them. He splits every hook into two parts.

## How to apply it
1. **Call-out.** Signal who it is for so the right person turns around, the way you notice your own name across a loud room (the "cocktail party effect"). Make it narrow enough to catch the right people and wide enough to catch as many of them as possible.
2. **Condition for value.** Promise, openly or by implication, that consuming the piece will pay off: if you (target) do this (watch/read), you get this (result). People keep watching only when they expect the benefit to beat the cost of their attention.
3. **Choose a verbal format** to get variety: labels (name the avatar), yes/no or open questions, conditionals (if you are X, then...), commands (read this if...), statements, lists/steps, stories, and exclamations or provocative lines.
4. **Layer a nonverbal hook** where the platform allows it: a visual or a sound that interrupts, used together with the words.

## When to reach for it
- Ads, content or outbound get impressions but few clicks or replies.
- Ad costs are climbing and spend will not scale.
- You are trying to reach a colder or new market profitably.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Ignoring the hook while polishing everything else. He tells you to spend most of your creative time on it (in his example, 8 of every 10 hours).
- Hooks that flatter the seller instead of speaking to the viewer. In Dean Graziosi's Larry King story, the ad bombed until only the opening sentence was re-recorded.
- Weak hooks mean fewer and worse customers, higher acquisition costs, and a ceiling on spend.

## The math
None given as a formula. His claim is that better hooks alone can bring 5x to 100x more attention for the same effort.
