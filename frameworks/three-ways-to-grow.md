# Three Ways to Grow (and the Pricing Genie)
slug: three-ways-to-grow · category: scaling · aliases: more customers / more per purchase / more purchases, business genie, pricing is the biggest profit lever, lifetime gross profit (LTGP)

## What it is
A business can only grow by getting more customers, raising what each purchase is worth, or getting customers to buy more times (the last two together mean making each customer worth more). His "business genie" thought experiment asks which one to double if you can only pick one. Doubling price wins by a wide margin because the extra revenue arrives with no extra acquisition or delivery cost.

## How to apply it
1. Know your numbers: new customers per month, price, churn (or purchase count), gross margin, cost to acquire (CAC). Measure lifetime value as gross profit, not revenue.
2. Find your ceiling: new customers per month x lifetime value; for subscriptions, steady-state customers = new per month / monthly churn.
3. Model doubling each lever and compare profit, not revenue.
4. Work pricing first: it is the lowest-effort, highest-return lever ([[instant-profit-pricing-playbook]], [[value-based-pricing]]).

## When to reach for it
- Revenue has plateaued and you do not know which lever to pull.
- You are about to spend heavily on acquisition while prices have not moved in years.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Counting revenue instead of gross profit when judging a customer's worth.
- Assuming the three doublings are equivalent because each doubles revenue.
- Chasing more customers first when price is the cheaper lever.

## The math
- Ceiling: 10 new clients/mo x $1,000 LTV = $10,000/mo maximum.
- LTV (gross profit): $1,000/mo x 90% margin x 5 months = $4,500.
- Genie base case: 30 new/mo, 33% monthly churn, $100/mo, 100 customers, $10,000 revenue, 20% net margin = $2,000 profit.
  - Double customers (CAC halves to $50): 200 customers, $20,000 revenue, about $7,000 profit = 3.5x.
  - Double purchases (churn 16.5%): 200 customers, $20,000 revenue, $7,000 profit = 3.5x.
  - Double price: same 100 customers, $20,000 revenue, costs still $8,000, $12,000 profit = 6x.
- He cites a Profitwell study of 512 companies: a 1% pricing improvement lifted profit about twice as much as a 1% retention improvement and nearly four times as much as acquisition.
