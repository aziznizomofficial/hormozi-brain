# Give:Ask Ratio and Give Until They Ask
slug: give-ask-ratio · category: content · aliases: jab jab jab right hook, give until they ask, integrated vs intermittent offers, monetising an audience

## What it is
Hormozi's model for making money from an audience without shrinking it. Rewarding content deposits goodwill; offers withdraw it. More goodwill means more attention and more compliance when you do ask. He builds on Gary Vaynerchuk's "jab, jab, jab, right hook" and pushes it further: keep giving until people ask you how to buy.

## How to apply it
- Benchmark the floor: TV runs about 47 minutes of content to 13 of ads (roughly 3.5:1) and Facebook about four posts per ad. Those are mature platforms maximising revenue. Growing platforms give almost everything and rarely ask; copy them if you want growth.
- Preferred mode: give in public, ask in private. Over-deliver until people DM, email or visit your site to buy. These tend to be the best customers.
- If you must ask, treat asks as commercials inside your own programme. Two ways:
  - Integrated: a short ask inside each piece, e.g. a few 30-second spots in an hour-long podcast, placed after a high-value moment or at the end. Best on long-form platforms.
  - Intermittent: a run of pure-value posts, then one promotional post (e.g. every eleventh). Best on short-form platforms.
- Every ask promotes one of two things: the core offer (built from the value equation, with a guarantee and a clear CTA) or a lead magnet related to the content, with the paid offer shown on the thank-you page. Unsure? Use the lead magnet; it is lower risk.
- After asking, go straight back to giving.

## When to reach for it
- An audience is growing but not producing revenue.
- Growth has stalled after you started promoting.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Over-asking. A friend's fast-growing podcast shrank once he loaded it with offers.
- Asking too early. He argues that every ask slows growth, so the longer you wait, the bigger the eventual payoff (p. 133).

## The math
Keep give:ask well above about 3:1. His podcast example: 1.5 minutes of asks in 60 minutes.
