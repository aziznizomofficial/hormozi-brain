# More, Better, New
slug: more-better-new · category: scaling · aliases: core four on steroids, size of the pie fallacy, new placements/platforms/core four

## What it is
Hormozi's way to get more engaged leads from a running core four. In order: do **more** of what works, do it **better**, then do it somewhere **new**. Opening story: a $2M/year chiropractic business spending $30k/month on one platform, with no content or outreach, thought it had saturated a $15B industry; another owner in the niche had spent $30k across four platforms in one week.

## How to apply it
- **More (always first).** Turn volume to max. Even with zero improvements, doubling inputs produces more leads. His tool is the [[rule-of-100]].
- **Better.** Push volume until it "breaks" (CAC climbs too high), then test to fix the step where most leads drop off. See [[constraint-split-testing]].
- **New.** Only when the returns from more and better fall below what fresh effort elsewhere would earn. Order, nine times in ten: new placement (stories, shorts, a different ad slot on the same platform), then new platform, then an entirely new core four activity. The rule for choosing: most leads for the amount of work.
- Then scale the new thing with more and better, and repeat. More and better work best together.
- **Fund experiments (pp. 355–357).** Reserve a slice of ad spend (1–10%) for untested ideas as paid education. After hearing this, he tripled his budget; Gym Launch went from $400k to $780k a month, then passed $4M as he tested each next lever (new audiences, text follow-up, more ads, affiliates). You either win or you learn.

## When to reach for it
A business at roughly $1–3M a year that has one channel working and believes it has "tapped out" the market. His first diagnostic question before investing in any company: what stops them from doing ten times their current volume? If nothing, do more; if something, make that better first.

## Mistakes he warns about
- **The Size of the Pie Fallacy**: mistaking the tiny slice you advertise to (one method, one platform, one format, a few competitors) for the entire market. He argues owners find "I'm as big as I can get" easier to say than admitting their advertising is weaker than they thought.
- Jumping to "new" before exhausting "more" and "better"; new is much harder in practice.

## The math
A $3M weight-loss owner feared that spending more than $40k/month would saturate a platform with over a billion daily users, selling into a $60B US industry. Quote: "There are more leads out there than you can possibly imagine." (p. 232)
