# Ad Callouts (the first-seconds hook)
slug: ad-callouts · category: marketing · aliases: call outs, hook, headline, cocktail party effect, first five seconds

## What it is
The callout is whatever gets the right person to notice the ad. Hormozi calls it the most important part of any ad by a wide margin, and says his advertising became roughly 20x more effective once he put most of his effort into the first five seconds. It runs on the "cocktail party effect": in a noisy room, you still turn around the instant you hear your own name. Every second of the ad exists to sell the next second, and the opening is the first sale.

## How to apply it
Aim for callouts narrow enough to hit the right people, wide enough to hit as many of them as possible.

**Verbal callouts (words):**
- **Labels** that put people in a group they identify with (role, trait, place). Local ads: the more local the better; "local area + type of person" is his all-time favourite.
- **Yes-questions** where answering "that's me" qualifies them.
- **If-then statements**: if you meet this condition, here is what to do.
- **Ridiculous results**: rare, strange, desirable outcomes.

**Nonverbal callouts (setting and spokesperson):**
- **Contrast**: bright colours, motion, attractive people, anything that sticks out early.
- **Likeness**: show people who look, talk and act like the buyer ("quack like a duck"). Broad market = diverse cast; narrow market = people who match. Mascots and non-founder spokespeople make the business less dependent on you, so more sellable.
- **The scene**: act out the yes-question visually (someone tossing in bed calls out bad sleepers; a local landmark calls out locals).

## When to reach for it
Any ad, organic hook, or piece of content that must stop a scroll. This is the part to test most.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Muting or skipping ads. He never does; treat every ad as a free lesson in how others call out your audience.
- Spending effort evenly across the ad instead of front-loading it.

## The math
One quote: "the headline is the first sale." (p. 197). Most test volume goes to the opening.
