# Client Financed Acquisition
slug: client-financed-acquisition · category: money-model · aliases: CFA, 30-day cash, customers pay for their own acquisition, 30-day break-even

## What it is
Many businesses have a healthy LTGP:CAC ratio yet still cannot scale ads, because the first purchase earns less than the cost of getting the customer and the rest trickles in over months. That is a cash-flow trap. Client financed acquisition means a new customer pays you more than it cost to acquire **and** fulfil them within the first 30 days, so every customer funds the next one. Hormozi calls it the key to unlimited scale.

## How to apply it
1. Measure gross profit collected per customer in their first 30 days versus CAC plus fulfilment cost.
2. If 30-day profit is below CAC, close the gap by selling more stuff immediately: an upsell, add-on, or upfront option right after the first purchase.
3. Why 30 days: any business can get roughly 30 days of interest-free money through a credit card. Recover the cost inside that window, clear the balance, and repeat with no debt and a new customer.
4. Once achieved, money stops being the constraint and ad spend can grow as fast as the rest of the business can absorb.

## When to reach for it
Ads are profitable on paper but the bank account shrinks as you scale; a subscription or low-ticket front end with a slow payback.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Refusing a good trade because payback is slow. A 3.3:1 customer is still worth taking; fix the cash flow rather than walking away.
- Treating the first sale as the whole model. What you sell next is unlimited.

## The math
- $15/month membership, $5 delivery = $10/month gross profit; 10-month stay = $100 LTGP; $30 CAC = 3.3:1 ratio, but only $10 back in month one, so two months to break even.
- Add a $100 upsell at full margin taken by 1 in 5 customers = $20 extra per customer. First-30-day profit becomes $30 = CAC. Customers are now effectively free and the remaining $10/month is surplus.
- He says this is how he took every company he started over the prior seven years past $1M/month within twelve months, without outside funding.
