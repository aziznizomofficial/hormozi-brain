# Classic Upsell
slug: classic-upsell · category: money-model · aliases: You Can't Have X Without Y, fries-with-that upsell, next-problem upsell, say no to say yes

## What it is
Every purchase solves one problem and reveals the next one. The Classic Upsell offers the fix for that next problem at the moment the customer notices it: no car without insurance, no bike without a helmet, no workout plan without nutrition. Existing buyers are far likelier to buy than strangers, and with good timing customers practically upsell themselves. Upsells often carry most of a business's profit. The first product usually isn't where the money is made.

## How to apply it
1. List what a customer always needs right after buying from you, then offer it immediately, in exchange for money.
2. Offer the higher-margin upsell first.
3. Phrase the close as "you don't want anything else, do you?" (p. 81). People are conditioned to say no, and here "no" is agreement.
4. Get the upsell into their hands as early as possible. Access sooner feels more valuable, and giving something back is harder than declining it.
5. Build upsells into how you deliver the core product (supplement suggestions inside meal plans, software recommendations inside training).
6. Use a free bonus that creates a paid need, like free earmuffs that then need paid storage.
7. Bundle several upsells into one named package ("Fastest Results") so one ask sells many items.
8. BAMFAM: end every meeting by booking the next, because each meeting is another chance to offer.
9. Charge for guarantees, warranties or insurance (5–50% on top) instead of including them free.

## When to reach for it
A "business" that sells one thing is really just a front end. Also: customers in a hyper-buying window (weddings, babies, new hobbies, moves) spend heavily for a short time.

## Mistakes he warns about
Upsells fail when the offer isn't wanted (too unrelated), comes too early (before the problem is felt), or isn't believed. The worst outcome is someone who would have said yes and was never asked.

## The math
- A burger shop making $0.25 on a $2 burger can reach about $3.00 per order with fries, drink and supersize, roughly 12x (pp. 76–77).
- Decoy sizing: priced $5/$8/$9, medium buyers move up to large. Priced $6/$7/$9, small buyers move up to medium (pp. 83–84).
- An art studio charging 10% for damage replacement it used to give free: 30% of customers pay for it (p. 84).
