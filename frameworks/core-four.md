# The Core Four
slug: core-four · category: leads · aliases: four ways to advertise, warm/cold x one-to-one/one-to-many

## What it is
Hormozi's claim that there are only four ways one person can let others know about anything. Two audiences: warm (people who have given you permission to contact them, e.g. friends, followers, past and current customers) and cold (people who have not, e.g. strangers, other people's audiences, bought lists). Two modes: one-to-one (private: a call, an email) and one-to-many (public: a post, a billboard, a podcast). Crossing them gives:
- one-to-one, warm: warm outreach
- one-to-many, warm: posting content
- one-to-one, cold: cold outreach
- one-to-many, cold: paid ads

## How to apply it
- Classify every current lead activity into one of the four boxes. Gaps are obvious immediately.
- Start where cost and skill are lowest (warm outreach) and move toward methods that need more skill and money, which the book orders on purpose.
- Remember automation does not change the category: blasting an email list is still one-to-one, just done fast by a machine.
- If lead flow is short, the diagnosis is always one of two things: not enough volume on the core four, or not enough skill at them.
- Sequencing (pp. 244–245): after warm outreach seeds the first customers, go to content if you have more time than money; go to cold outreach or ads if you have more money than time. You only need one to succeed: pick it, max it out, then more/better/new. The methods compound, so content makes ads work better, outreach works warm leads better, and so on. He built his businesses on different pairs (e.g. gyms on content plus ads, Gym Launch on ads plus cold outreach, Acquisition.com on content).
- Once your hours run out, the next step is getting other people to run the four for you (see [[lead-getters]]).

## When to reach for it
- Planning a marketing strategy from scratch.
- An owner feels lost in channel noise (TikTok vs SEO vs podcasts); map it back to the four.
- Diagnosing why leads dried up.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Treating automation as a fifth method.
- Complaining about lead flow while doing too little of the four, or doing them badly.

## The math
Output depends on volume times skill across the four methods. He later ties this to his single rule of doing more, better, and new (see [[more-better-new]]).
