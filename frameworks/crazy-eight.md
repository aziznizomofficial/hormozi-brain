# The Crazy Eight
slug: crazy-eight · category: pricing · aliases: 8 ways to increase LTV, make customers worth more, monetization levers

## What it is
Hormozi's checklist for raising lifetime gross profit. He found the standard answer (raise order value or purchase frequency) too broad to act on, so he broke it into eight levers he drills his teams on until they can apply them to any product. His favorite upsell sits among them: more of what they just bought, or more help with it, delivered faster, with less risk, effort or hassle, at a higher price. In one acquisition, adding that upsell multiplied LTV by 2.2 and allowed 5x ad spend.

## How to apply it
Run all eight for your business and write an action for each:
1. **Raise prices.** Same thing, more money (see [[price-testing]]).
2. **Lower delivery cost.** Raise the client-to-staff ratio, offshore talent, productize for similar customers, move from done-for-you to done-with-you, cap usage (e.g., revisions), switch lifetime access to annual, go remote, delete recurring meetings quarterly, buy in bulk or prepay vendors for 10–20% off.
3. **Upsell frequency, so they buy again later.** Add a recurring version, reduce churn, and run quarterly follow-up promotions to past customers and leads.
4. **Upsell quantity, so they buy more now.** Bulk (prepay the year), more often (monthly → every 3 weeks), or bigger (1 hour → 3 hours per visit).
5. **Upsell quality, so they buy a premium version.** Newer, or better ingredients, materials, people, speed, access, live, in person, personalized, done-for-you.
6. **Downsell quantity.** Fewer, smaller or less frequent, rather than nothing.
7. **Downsell quality.** Same result with a worse experience (slower, more junior staff, recorded, DIY, no guarantee).
8. **Cross-sell** a complementary product (snow removal for lawn clients, fries with the burger).

## When to reach for it
Ad costs are climbing, a one-product business has a front end but no back end, or you need more cash from the customers you already have.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Cross-selling something your customers don't want. He once had to survey buyers to stop founders from adding an unrelated passion product, and customers overwhelmingly chose the closely related one.
- Offering downsells to qualified buyers. He forbids reps from doing this so the main offer isn't cannibalized. Downsell only people who don't qualify for the main offer.
- Picking changes that break operations. Choose ones that fit your existing infrastructure.

## The math
Added LTV from a cross-sell = take rate × gross profit of the add-on. With a $100 LTV and 20% taking a $100 all-profit add-on, LTV becomes $120 (p. 17). Quantity and quality upsells offered first routinely lift cash collected by 20% or more (pp. 18, 20).
