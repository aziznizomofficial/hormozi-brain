# Speed to Lead (Three Speeds)
slug: speed-to-lead · category: leads · aliases: speed to contact, 5-minute rule, 72-hour rule, pull appointments forward, hot handoff, speed of response

## What it is
The second pillar of lead nurture. A prospect's motivation is high only for a short window after they show interest, so the business that reaches them first and meets them soonest wins. Hormozi splits speed into three parts and argues that the idle gaps created by more availability should be spent calling new leads at once. A side benefit: leads reached faster need fewer touches before they buy.

## How to apply it
1. **Speed to first contact:** reach every new lead within 5 minutes (extra credit: under 60 seconds). His litmus test is that at least once a day a lead should comment on how fast you were.
2. **Speed to first appointment:** let people book no more than about 3 days out (5 at most) and aim for same day, next day or the day after. If a lead resists booking soon, treat it as an obstacle. Ask when they had planned to act and use that time.
3. **Pull appointments forward:** in spare moments, call people who booked themselves, confirm who they are, what they want and what they have tried, then offer an earlier opening (or start now). Each call ends one of five ways: no answer (clear the slot), unqualified (remove, and offer free help or a downsell), qualified and free now (sell now or do a hot handoff), moved earlier, or confirmed.
4. **Hot handoff:** if someone else closes, introduce them in a group text and build up the closer's credibility first.
5. **Speed of response:** answer questions between booking and appointment within minutes. Track reply time by rep, since this is where leads fall through.

## When to reach for it
- Leads are called hours or days later.
- The calendar is booked out one to two weeks or more.
- No-shows cluster on appointments set far in the future.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Letting people book far out. You get more bookings but more ghosts, a triple loss: the no-show, a demoralised sales team, and a slot someone else could have used.
- Treating speed as optional overhead. The cost is spare capacity, and the return is several times that.

## The math
Statistics he cites: roughly 391% more conversions when contacted within the first minute (Velocify); contact within an hour made qualification 7x more likely than an hour later and 60x more likely than after 24 hours (HBR); 78% of buyers go with the first responder (Lead Connect). He contrasts reaching a lead in 42 seconds with the 42-hour average reply time (InsideSales.com).
