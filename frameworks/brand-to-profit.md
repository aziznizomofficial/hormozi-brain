# Brand to Profit (Turning Associations into Premium Prices)
slug: brand-to-profit · category: marketing · aliases: translating brand into sales, branded vs unbranded, pricing power, premium branded product, big brand = big profits

## What it is
Hormozi's step-by-step account of how a brand turns into money. Associations that your ideal customer values rub off onto your logo. The logo then turns a commodity into a premium product that buyers want to be associated with, and they pay for that association. Following Warren Buffett, he treats pricing power as the clearest test of a good business. A brand raises all three drivers of business value together: cheaper customers (more people convert from the same ads), more valuable customers (higher prices, repeat buying), and lower risk (loyalty that keeps it going).

## How to apply it
The nine-link chain:
1. Start with a mark that means nothing yet. People can tell it is a brand but not what it stands for.
2. Pair it with people, content and outcomes your ideal customer likes.
3. The mark starts to stand for things the customer likes.
4. Take a generic product (his example is a hat).
5. Put the brand on it, making it a premium branded product.
6. Customers want the association.
7. They pay money for the product.
8. They get the product *and* the identity ("now I'm an ambitious entrepreneur").
9. You earn higher profit from the higher price.

Profit test: the brand has done its job if the extra margin from premium pricing covers what the pairings cost you (content, people, experiences).

## When to reach for it
- You sell something close to a commodity and compete on price.
- Advertising returns are thin, and a competitor 10% cheaper steals your customers.
- You are deciding whether to invest in content, partnerships or experiences that will not pay back immediately.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Putting all the budget into product and commissions and none into being cool. His supplement brand paid more and was better, yet could not win over an affiliate friend.
- Expecting instant payback. Branded returns are huge but slow, which is why most people skip it.

## The math
His table, with the same cost of goods ($500) and ad spend ($2,000):
- **Unbranded:** $2,500 price → $2,000 gross profit per sale; 1% CTR = 100 clicks; 4% conversion = 2 sales; $8,000 gross profit; 4:1 return.
- **Branded:** $5,500 price → $5,000 per sale; 3% CTR = 300 clicks; 6% conversion = 18 sales; $90,000 gross profit; 45:1 return.
He sums up the gap as about 4.5x the sales and 11x the return on ad spend. His prose also calls the unbranded price $2,000, which conflicts with the table.
