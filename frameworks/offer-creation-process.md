# Offer Creation Process (Problems to Solutions, Trim & Stack)
slug: offer-creation-process · category: offer · aliases: five steps to a grand slam offer, problems and solutions list, trim and stack, value stack, brick exercise, divergent thinking

## What it is
A five-step method for building a bundle so complete that it cannot be compared. You map every obstacle between the buyer and their result, turn each into a promised solution, brainstorm many ways to deliver each, then keep only what is high value for its cost and package the survivors as a named stack. It relies on divergent thinking (many answers to one problem, like listing uses for a brick) rather than school-style single-answer thinking.

## How to apply it
1. Identify the dream outcome: sell the destination, not the vehicle (weight loss, not a membership).
2. List problems in the order the customer meets them, including what happens right before and after using your product. For each step, check the four value-driver objections: not worth it, won't work for me, too hard, takes too long. Aim for dozens.
3. Turn each problem into a solution, usually by writing "how to" plus the reversed problem.
4. Brainstorm delivery vehicles for every solution (see [[delivery-cube]]), including things you would never actually do.
5. Trim and stack: cut high-cost/low-value items first, then low-cost/low-value. Keep low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value. Favor one-to-many assets (templates, tools, recordings) that cost once and deliver forever. Then bundle into named components, each with a benefit and a stated value.

## When to reach for it
- Your offer is "a membership" or "hours of service" and prospects compare it to cheaper options.
- A single objection keeps killing sales.
- Launching your first offer: over-deliver heavily, then streamline once cash flows.

## Mistakes he warns about
- Refusing to solve an objection your way (he lost sales insisting clients never eat out, until he built an eating-out guide).
- Leaving any perceived problem unsolved; one missing piece can stop a purchase.
- Getting overwhelmed by a long problem list; more problems means more value to sell.

## The math
His weight-loss stack listed seven named components with a combined stated value of $4,351, sold for $599; facilities later sold longer versions for $2,400-5,200. A spreadsheet tool took about 100 hours to build and then produced personalized meal plans in about 15 minutes each.
